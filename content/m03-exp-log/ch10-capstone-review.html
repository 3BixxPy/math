Synthesis — concepts combining, not just shuffled

Two problems that each need multiple chapters' tools working together, not just one technique per problem.

Synthesis 1
Ch. 3 — transformationsCh. 4 — log-exp inverse

The transformed exponential f(x) = 2^(x-3) + 1 is straight out of Chapter 3. Find its inverse algebraically (Chapter 4's swap-and-solve method), and state the inverse's domain by connecting it to f's own range — the same domain-swaps-with-range relationship from Module 1, Chapter 11.

Step 1 — read off f's asymptote and range (Chapter 3)

\(h=3,\ k=1\), so f's horizontal asymptote is \(y=1\). Since the base is \(2>1\) (growth), the graph stays entirely above its asymptote: range \(y>1\).

Step 2 — swap x and y

\(y=2^{x-3}+1\) → swap: \(x=2^{y-3}+1\) → isolate the exponential: \(x-1=2^{y-3}\)

Step 3 — convert to log form and solve for y (Chapter 4's definition)

\(\log_2(x-1) = y-3 \Rightarrow y = \log_2(x-1)+3\)

Result — the domain/range connection

\(f^{-1}(x)=\log_2(x-1)+3\), domain \(x>1\) (the log's argument must be positive). Notice \(x>1\) is exactly f's range from Step 1 — domain of f⁻¹ equals range of f, the same relationship Module 1, Chapter 11 established for every inverse pair, now confirmed on an exponential/log pair specifically.

Synthesis 2
Ch. 6 — product ruleCh. 7 — power ruleCh. 9 — domain check

Solve for x: 2·log₂(x) = log₂(x+6) + log₂(3).

Step 1 — apply the power rule to the left side (Chapter 7)

\(2\log_2(x) = \log_2(x^2)\)

Step 2 — apply the product rule to the right side (Chapter 6)

\(\log_2(x+6)+\log_2(3) = \log_2\big(3(x+6)\big) = \log_2(3x+18)\)

Step 3 — same base, same log value, so the arguments must be equal

\(\log_2(x^2)=\log_2(3x+18) \Rightarrow x^2=3x+18 \Rightarrow x^2-3x-18=0 \Rightarrow (x-6)(x+3)=0 \Rightarrow x=6 \text{ or } x=-3\)

Result — the domain check (Chapter 9)

Original equation needs \(x>0\). \(x=6\): valid. \(x=-3\): \(\log_2(-3)\) is undefined — extraneous, reject it. Final answer: \(x=6\).
