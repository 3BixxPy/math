Evaluating Definite Integrals — Practice

No new tool this chapter. FTC2 is now the whole method — what's left is fluency: applying it cleanly across polynomial, trig, and exponential integrands, without a sign error creeping in.

Why this matters

Chapter 7 introduced the formula; this chapter is where it becomes automatic. The mechanics never change — find an antiderivative, evaluate at the top, evaluate at the bottom, subtract — but the integrands are about to get varied enough, and the arithmetic careful enough, that fluency is worth building deliberately rather than assuming it for free.

Worked example — a trig integrand

Evaluate ∫[0,π] sin(x) dx.

Step 1 — antiderivative (Chapter 2)

\(F(x)=-\cos x\)

Step 2 — apply FTC2 (8.7.1)

\(\Big[-\cos x\Big]_0^\pi = -\cos\pi - (-\cos0) = -(-1) - (-1) = 1+1 = 2\)

Worked example — an exponential integrand

Evaluate ∫[0,1] eˣ dx.

Step 1 — antiderivative (Chapter 2)

\(F(x)=e^x\)

Step 2 — apply FTC2

\(\Big[e^x\Big]_0^1 = e^1-e^0 = e-1\)

Common mistake

Sign errors in F(b) − F(a), especially when F(a) itself is negative — a genuine Module 0 signed-arithmetic callback. Evaluate ∫[−2,1] x dx: \(F(x)=x^2/2\), so \(F(1)=0.5\) and \(F(-2)=(-2)^2/2=2\). The answer is \(F(1)-F(-2)=0.5-2=-1.5\). The two places this goes wrong: miscomputing \(F(-2)\) itself as \(-2\) instead of \(2\) (forgetting that squaring a negative kills the sign before halving), or computing \(0.5-2\) correctly but then "fixing" the negative result by hand, as if a negative answer must be a mistake. It isn't — a negative definite integral just means net signed area below the axis (Chapter 5), a perfectly legitimate answer.

Practice

  1. Evaluate ∫[0,3] x² dx.
    \(\big[x^3/3\big]_0^3=9\)
  2. Evaluate ∫[0,π/2] sin(x) dx.
    \(\big[-\cos x\big]_0^{\pi/2}=0-(-1)=1\)
  3. Evaluate ∫[0,2] eˣ dx.
    \(\big[e^x\big]_0^2=e^2-1\)
  4. Evaluate ∫[−1,2] (2x+1) dx.
    \(\big[x^2+x\big]_{-1}^{2}=(4+2)-(1-1)=6\)
  5. Evaluate ∫[1,2] 3/x dx.
    \(\big[3\ln|x|\big]_1^2=3\ln2\)
  6. Evaluate ∫[−3,3] x³ dx.
    \(0\) — odd function, symmetric interval, cancels exactly.
  7. bonus — Evaluate ∫[0,π] cos(x) dx.
    \(\big[\sin x\big]_0^\pi=0-0=0\)
  8. bonus — Evaluate ∫[−1,0] eˣ dx.
    \(\big[e^x\big]_{-1}^{0}=1-e^{-1}=1-\tfrac1e\)
  9. bonus — Evaluate ∫[1,4] √x dx.
    \(\big[\tfrac23 x^{3/2}\big]_1^4 = \tfrac{16}{3}-\tfrac23=\tfrac{14}{3}\)
  10. bonus — A student evaluates ∫[−3,2] x dx, gets F(2)−F(−3) = 2−4.5 = −2.5, then drops the negative sign "because area can't be negative." What's wrong with that reasoning, and what's the correct final answer?
    The correct answer is \(-2.5\) — the sign is meaningful (Chapter 5's signed area), not an error to erase. This interval has more below-axis contribution than above-axis, and the integral is supposed to reflect that.
  11. challenge — Evaluate ∫[0,4] (x−2) dx. Before computing, predict the sign using Chapter 5's signed-area idea (where is x−2 negative vs. positive on [0,4]?), then verify.
    \(x-2<0\) on \([0,2)\) and \(>0\) on \((2,4]\) — symmetric around \(x=2\), so the two triangular pieces should cancel: predict \(0\). Verify: \(\big[x^2/2-2x\big]_0^4=(8-8)-0=0\) ✓.
