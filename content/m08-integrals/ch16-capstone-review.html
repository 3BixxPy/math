Module 8 Capstone — Mixed Review & Diagnostic Retake

Fifteen chapters, shuffled together, plus a look back at the very first diagnostic — where calculus itself was a blank page, let alone its reverse operation.

Why mixed, not grouped by chapter

Same reasoning as every capstone since Module 0: a problem that doesn't announce its own chapter is the real test of whether an idea moved into durable memory, not just short-term pattern matching against a chapter title. This module in particular built two threads — antiderivatives (Chapters 1–2) and definite integrals (Chapters 3–5) — that stayed deliberately separate until Chapters 6–7 revealed they were the same thing all along. Shuffling the review is one more chance to notice how thoroughly that fusion held.

Mixed review (all of Module 8)

  1. Given ∫[0,4] f = 6 and ∫[4,9] f = −2, find ∫[0,9] f.
    \(6+(-2)=4\) (Chapter 5, additivity).
  2. Find the area between y = x² and y = 4x.
    Intersections: \(x^2=4x\Rightarrow x=0,4\); line on top. \(\int_0^4(4x-x^2)dx=\big[2x^2-x^3/3\big]_0^4=32-64/3=32/3\) (Chapter 12).
  3. Evaluate ∫(6x² + cos x) dx.
    \(2x^3+\sin x+C\) (Chapter 2).
  4. Evaluate ∫2x(x² + 5)³ dx.
    \(u=x^2+5\): \(\dfrac{(x^2+5)^4}{4}+C\) (Chapter 9).
  5. Evaluate ∫[1,4] (2x − 1/x) dx.
    \(\big[x^2-\ln|x|\big]_1^4=(16-\ln4)-(1-0)=15-\ln4\) (Chapters 2 & 7).
  6. With 3 rectangles, find the left-endpoint estimate for f(x) = x on [0, 3].
    \(\Delta x=1\); left edges \(0,1,2\); heights \(0,1,2\); sum \(=3\) (Chapter 3).
  7. Evaluate ∫[0,3] x² dx via FTC2.
    \(9\) (Chapter 7).
  8. If v(t) = 4t (m/s), find the net displacement from t = 0 to t = 2.
    \(\big[2t^2\big]_0^2=8\) m (Chapter 14).
  9. Find the general antiderivative of f(x) = x⁴.
    \(\dfrac{x^5}{5}+C\) (Chapter 1).
  10. Evaluate ∫[0,1] 2x(x² + 3)² dx, converting the bounds.
    \(u=x^2+3\), bounds \(3\to4\): \(\big[u^3/3\big]_3^4=64/3-27/3=37/3\) (Chapter 10).
  11. If g(x) = ∫[0,x] (t³ + 2) dt, find g′(x).
    \(x^3+2\) — directly by FTC1 (Chapter 6).
  12. Evaluate ∫3x²eˣ³ dx.
    \(u=x^3\): \(e^{x^3}+C\) (Chapter 15).
  13. Write (don't evaluate) the right-endpoint Riemann sum limit for ∫[0,5] x³ dx.
    \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^n\left(\frac{5i}{n}\right)^3\frac{5}{n}\) (Chapter 4).
  14. Evaluate ∫[0,π] cos(x) dx.
    \(\big[\sin x\big]_0^\pi=0\) (Chapter 8).
  15. Find the average value of f(x) = 3x² on [0, 2].
    \(\frac12\int_0^23x^2dx=\frac12(8)=4\) (Chapter 13).
  16. Evaluate ∫sin(2x) dx.
    \(u=2x\): \(-\dfrac12\cos(2x)+C\) (Chapter 11).

Synthesis — concepts combining, not just shuffled

Two problems that each need multiple chapters' tools working together, not just one technique per problem.

Synthesis 1
Ch. 12 — area between curvesCh. 9 — u-substitution

Find the area of the region bounded by y = x√(x² + 4), the x-axis, and the vertical line x = √5, for x in [0, √5].

Step 1 — set up as an area-between-curves integral (Ch. 12), with the x-axis as "bottom"

Since \(x\sqrt{x^2+4}\ge0\) on \([0,\sqrt5]\), the curve is "top" and \(y=0\) is "bottom": \(\displaystyle\text{Area}=\int_0^{\sqrt5} x\sqrt{x^2+4}\,dx\). The integrand isn't a bare power — it needs Chapter 9's technique before it can actually be integrated.

Step 2 — substitute (Ch. 9) and convert the bounds (Ch. 10)

\(u=x^2+4,\ du=2x\,dx\Rightarrow x\,dx=du/2\). Bounds: \(x=0\Rightarrow u=4\); \(x=\sqrt5\Rightarrow u=9\).

Step 3 — integrate entirely in u

\(\displaystyle\frac12\int_4^9\sqrt u\,du = \frac12\Big[\frac23u^{3/2}\Big]_4^9 = \frac13\big(27-8\big) = \frac{19}{3}\)

Result

Area \(=\dfrac{19}{3}\). The area-between-curves setup is pure Chapter 12; actually carrying out the integration needed Chapter 9's substitution — neither chapter alone gets there.

Synthesis 2
Ch. 6 — FTC Part 1Ch. 7 — FTC Part 2Ch. 9 — u-substitution

Let g(x) = ∫[0,x] t·cos(t²) dt. Find g′(x). Then evaluate g(√(π/2)) exactly.

Step 1 — g′(x), via FTC1 (Ch. 6) — no integration needed at all

\(g'(x)=x\cos(x^2)\), read straight off the integrand by (8.6.1). Notice: actually integrating \(t\cos(t^2)\) would require Chapter 9's u-substitution — but FTC1 makes that entirely unnecessary just to find \(g'\).

Step 2 — but evaluating g itself does need to integrate, so substitute (Ch. 9)

\(u=t^2,\ du=2t\,dt\Rightarrow t\,dt=du/2\): \(\displaystyle\int t\cos(t^2)\,dt=\frac12\int\cos u\,du=\frac12\sin u+C=\frac12\sin(t^2)+C\).

Step 3 — evaluate via FTC2 (Ch. 7) from 0 to √(π/2)

\(\Big[\frac12\sin(t^2)\Big]_0^{\sqrt{\pi/2}} = \frac12\sin\!\left(\frac\pi2\right)-\frac12\sin(0) = \frac12(1)-0=\frac12\)

Result

g′(x) = x cos(x²) (immediate, Chapter 6) — while g(√(π/2)) = 1/2 (needs Chapters 7 and 9 together). Same function g, two genuinely different questions, two different toolkits.

Diagnostic retake

On day one of this course, calculus was a clean slate — the original diagnostic had no section for it at all. Sixteen chapters later, differentiation and integration are visibly the same coin, thanks to the Fundamental Theorem.

1. Find the general antiderivative of f(x) = 6x².

Originally: this idea didn't exist for you yet — no calculus section on the diagnostic.

\(2x^3+C\). See Chapters 1–2.

2. Evaluate ∫[0,2] (3x² + 1) dx using FTC2.

Originally: no calculus section on the diagnostic.

\(\big[x^3+x\big]_0^2=8+2=10\). See Chapter 7.

3. A ball is thrown so its velocity is v(t) = −32t + 64 (ft/s) for t in [0, 2] seconds. Before computing anything, predict whether the net displacement over [0, 2] will be positive, negative, or zero — then verify by evaluating ∫[0,2] v(t)dt.

Originally: no calculus section on the diagnostic.

\(v(t)\ge0\) throughout \([0,2]\) (it only reaches \(0\) exactly at \(t=2\)) — the ball is rising the whole interval, so displacement should be strongly positive. Verify: \(\big[-16t^2+64t\big]_0^2=(-64+128)-0=64\) ft. Matches the prediction. See Chapter 14.
Module 8 complete

From "what's the reverse of a derivative" to a full integration toolkit: antiderivatives and their rules, the area-estimation problem and formal Riemann sums, the definite integral and its properties, both halves of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, fluent FTC2 evaluation, u-substitution (indefinite and definite), the trig integral table, area between curves, average value, and the net change theorem — sixteen chapters that turned two completely independent ideas, antiderivatives and area, into one theorem.
