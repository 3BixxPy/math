Two Essential Trig Limits

The squeeze theorem (Chapter 10) exists largely because of these two limits — direct substitution gives 0/0 for both, and no algebraic trick from Chapter 5 (factoring, rationalizing) untangles them the way it did for polynomial and radical expressions.

Why this matters

These two specific limits will become the literal foundation of differentiating sin and cos the moment Module 6 begins — every derivative involving a trig function traces back to one of these two facts. They're worth memorizing cold now, the same way 90°=π/2 was worth memorizing in Module 2, not just understanding once and moving on.

Theorem — The two essential trig limits
\[ \lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x} = 1 \]
(5.11.1)
\[ \lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x} = 0 \]
(5.11.2)

Both follow from the squeeze theorem (Chapter 10), applied to a geometric argument comparing the area of a thin circular sector of angle x against the areas of two triangles that trap it from below and above — as the angle shrinks toward 0, those bounding areas squeeze sin(x)/x down to exactly 1. The full geometric proof is outside this course's scope; the one-paragraph sketch above is enough to know these aren't arbitrary facts — they're provable, and provable by the exact tool Chapter 10 just built.

Worked example — a coefficient inside the argument

Evaluate limx→0 sin(3x)/x.

Step 1 — this is NOT the same as (5.11.1) — there's a 3 inside the sine

\(\sin(3x)/x\) is not automatically \(1\); the "essential limit" only directly applies to \(\sin(\text{something})/\text{that exact same something}\). Multiply and divide by \(3\) to force that exact match: \(\dfrac{\sin(3x)}{x} = 3\cdot\dfrac{\sin(3x)}{3x}\).

Step 2 — substitute u = 3x

As \(x\to0\), \(u=3x\to0\) too, so \(\dfrac{\sin(3x)}{3x} = \dfrac{\sin u}{u} \to 1\) by (5.11.1).

Result

\(3\cdot1 = 3\).

Worked example — coefficient in the denominator instead

Evaluate limx→0 sin(x)/(2x).

Step 1 — pull the constant out front

\(\dfrac{\sin x}{2x} = \dfrac12\cdot\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\)

Result

\(\dfrac{\sin x}{x}\to1\) by (5.11.1), so the whole limit is \(\dfrac12\cdot1=\dfrac12\).

Common mistake

Forgetting to account for a coefficient inside the sine's argument. sin(3x)/x is not the same limit as sin(x)/x — the argument and the denominator have to match exactly before (5.11.1) applies directly. Whenever they don't, multiply and divide by whatever constant is needed to force the match (the substitution trick above), rather than assuming the coefficient doesn't matter.

Practice

  1. Evaluate limx→0 sin(2x)/x.
    \(2\)
  2. Evaluate limx→0 sin(x)/(4x).
    \(1/4\)
  3. Evaluate limx→0 sin(7x)/(2x).
    \(7/2\)
  4. Evaluate limx→0 (1−cos x)/(3x).
    \(\frac13\cdot\frac{1-\cos x}{x}\to\frac13(0)=0\)
  5. Evaluate limx→0 x/sin(x). (This is the reciprocal of the essential limit.)
    Since \(\sin(x)/x\to1\) (a nonzero value), its reciprocal's limit is also \(1\).
  6. Evaluate limx→0 sin(4x)/sin(2x). (Hint: multiply and divide by x, then handle each piece separately.)
    Rewrite as \(\dfrac{\sin(4x)/x}{\sin(2x)/x} = \dfrac{4\cdot[\sin(4x)/(4x)]}{2\cdot[\sin(2x)/(2x)]}\to\dfrac{4(1)}{2(1)}=2\).
  7. bonus — Evaluate limx→0 sin(x²)/x². (Treat x² as the "u.")
    \(1\) — same substitution idea, \(u=x^2\to0\) as \(x\to0\).
  8. bonus — Evaluate limx→0 tan(x)/x. (Hint: tan x = sin x / cos x.)
    \(\dfrac{\tan x}{x} = \dfrac{\sin x}{x}\cdot\dfrac{1}{\cos x}\to1\cdot\dfrac{1}{\cos0}=1\cdot1=1\).
  9. bonus — Evaluate limx→0 (1−cos(2x))/x. (Mirror the coefficient trick from the sin(kx) example.)
    \(\dfrac{1-\cos(2x)}{x} = 2\cdot\dfrac{1-\cos(2x)}{2x}\to2(0)=0\).
  10. bonus — Explain in one or two sentences why limx→0 sin(x)/x can't be evaluated by Chapter 4's direct-substitution laws or Chapter 5's factoring/rationalizing techniques.
    Direct substitution gives \(0/0\), so Chapter 4's laws don't apply as-is; and \(\sin x\) isn't a polynomial or radical expression, so there's nothing to factor or rationalize the way Chapter 5's techniques require — it genuinely needs a different tool, the squeeze theorem (Chapter 10).
  11. challenge — Evaluate limx→0 (sin(5x) − sin(3x))/x by splitting into two separate fractions and evaluating each with the essential limit.
    \(\dfrac{\sin(5x)}{x}-\dfrac{\sin(3x)}{x} = 5\cdot\dfrac{\sin(5x)}{5x} - 3\cdot\dfrac{\sin(3x)}{3x} \to 5(1)-3(1)=2\).
