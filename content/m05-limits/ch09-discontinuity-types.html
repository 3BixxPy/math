Chapter 9 · concept:discontinuity-classification

Types of Discontinuity

Chapter 8 gave the three-part test for continuity. This chapter names exactly how each way of failing it looks — "discontinuous" alone undersells just how differently a hole, a jump, and a blow-up actually behave.

Why this matters

This chapter doesn't introduce a single new technique — it's a naming exercise that ties together everything so far: Chapter 3's three limit-failure modes, Chapter 1's "f(a) doesn't have to be defined" idea, and Chapter 8's three-part test, all applied together. Knowing precisely which type of discontinuity you're looking at tells you immediately whether it's fixable (and how) or fundamentally not.

Definition — Three types of discontinuity
  • Removable (a "hole") — limx→a f(x) exists, but either f(a) is undefined or it doesn't match the limit. Condition (2) of (5.8.1) holds; (1) or (3) fails. Fixable by redefining f at the single point a to equal the limit.
  • Jump — the one-sided limits both exist but disagree (Chapter 2's/Chapter 3's disagreement case). Condition (2) fails outright — there's no single limit to even compare against f(a). No redefinition of one point can fix this; the two sides are approaching genuinely different values.
  • Infinite — unbounded behavior near a (Chapter 3's/Chapter 7's unbounded case). Condition (2) fails the same way — no finite limit exists. Also unfixable by redefining a single point; the whole neighborhood is blowing up.
Worked example — classify each
(a) f(x) = (x²−1)/(x−1) at x=1

Factor: \(\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1}=x+1\) for \(x\neq1\), so \(\lim_{x\to1}f(x)=2\), but \(f(1)\) is literally \(0/0\), undefined. The limit exists and \(f(a)\) doesn't match (it isn't even defined) — removable. Redefining \(f(1)=2\) patches it completely.

(b) g(x) = {x if x<0; x+2 if x≥0} at x=0

Left-hand limit \(\to0\); right-hand limit \(\to2\). They disagree — jump. No single value of \(g(0)\) could ever make both sides agree.

(c) h(x) = 1/(x−3) at x=3

One-sided limits are \(-\infty\) and \(+\infty\) (Chapter 7's sign analysis) — unbounded near \(x=3\) — infinite. No redefinition of a single point comes close to fixing unbounded growth throughout the whole neighborhood.

Common mistake

Confusing removable and jump discontinuities. The test that separates them: does redefining f at the single point a fix everything (removable), or does the function approach two fundamentally different values from the two sides, so that no single patched value could ever satisfy both directions at once (jump)? If a genuine two-sided limit exists at all (even if f(a) is wrong or missing), it's removable. If the one-sided limits themselves disagree, it's a jump — there's nothing to patch to.

Practice

  1. Classify: f(x)=(x²−4)/(x−2) at x=2.
    Removable — factors and cancels, limit exists (\(=4\)), \(f(2)\) undefined.
  2. Classify: g(x) = {3x if x≤1; x+1 if x>1} at x=1.
    Jump — left-hand limit \(3\), right-hand limit \(2\); they disagree.
  3. Classify: h(x)=1/(x+4) at x=−4.
    Infinite — unbounded near \(x=-4\) (denominator zero, numerator nonzero, doesn't cancel).
  4. Classify: k(x) = (x²−5x+6)/(x−3) at x=3. (Factor first.)
    \(x^2-5x+6=(x-3)(x-2)\), so it simplifies to \(x-2\); limit exists (\(=1\)), \(k(3)\) undefined — removable.
  5. Classify: m(x) = {x² if x<2; x²+5 if x≥2} at x=2.
    Jump — left-hand limit \(4\), right-hand limit \(9\); they disagree (even though \(m(2)=9\) matches the right side, the two-sided limit still doesn't exist).
  6. Classify: p(x)=1/(x−1)² at x=1.
    Infinite — unbounded (\(+\infty\)) on both sides.
  7. bonus — For f(x)=(x²−9)/(x−3) everywhere except f(3)=100, classify the discontinuity at x=3 and state exactly how to fix it.
    Removable — the limit is \(6\), but \(f(3)=100\) doesn't match. Fix by redefining \(f(3)=6\).
  8. bonus — Classify: g(x)=2x/(x−5) at x=5.
    Infinite — numerator (\(10\)) is nonzero at \(x=5\), denominator is zero, nothing cancels.
  9. bonus — True or false: every discontinuity of a rational function P(x)/Q(x) at a zero of Q is removable.
    False — only removable if the matching factor also appears in \(P\) and cancels; otherwise it's infinite.
  10. bonus — For h(x) = x+3 everywhere except h(2)=0, classify the discontinuity at x=2.
    Removable — \(\lim_{x\to2}h(x)=5\) exists, but \(h(2)=0\neq5\).
  11. challenge — Construct a single rational expression (one formula, not separate pieces) with a removable discontinuity at x=1 and an infinite discontinuity at x=−1. Explain which factors you needed and why.
    E.g. \(f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{(x-1)(x+1)}\), which simplifies to \(\dfrac{1}{x+1}\) for \(x\neq1\). The \((x-1)\) factor cancels — removable hole at \(x=1\). The \((x+1)\) factor survives in the denominator with nothing to cancel it — true infinite discontinuity (vertical asymptote) at \(x=-1\).
