Chapter 12 · concept:general-trig-solutions

General Solutions — Adding the Period

Chapter 11 restricted solutions to one trip around the circle. Remove that restriction — using Chapter 6 and 8's periodicity facts — and every trig equation actually has infinitely many solutions.

Why this matters

If sin θ = 1/2 at \(\theta=\pi/6\), it's also true at \(\theta=\pi/6+2\pi\), and \(\pi/6+4\pi\), and \(\pi/6-2\pi\) — every full trip around the circle lands on the same point (Chapter 6's periodicity). Chapter 11's restricted-interval answers were never the whole answer; they were one representative from each infinite family. This chapter writes down the whole family.

Rule — General solution schema (sin, cos)
\[ \theta = \theta_0 + 2\pi n, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z} \]
(2.12.1)

For each particular solution \(\theta_0\) found on [0, 2π) (Chapter 11), add \(2\pi n\) for any integer \(n\) (positive, negative, or zero) to sweep out every coterminal angle — because sine and cosine repeat every \(2\pi\) (equation 2.6.1). If an equation has two particular solutions on one period, it has two separate families, one built from each.

Rule — General solution schema (tan)
\[ \theta = \theta_0 + \pi n, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z} \]
(2.12.2)

Tangent's period is \(\pi\), not \(2\pi\) (equation 2.8.1) — so its general solution only needs one family per equation, built by adding \(\pi n\). This single family already covers what would otherwise be two separate \(+2\pi n\) families for sin/cos-type equations, because tangent repeats twice as often.

Worked example

Find the general solution of sin θ = 1/2.

Step 1 — particular solutions on [0, 2π) (Chapter 11)

Reference angle \(\pi/6\), sine positive in QI and QII: \(\theta_0=\pi/6\) or \(\theta_0=5\pi/6\).

Step 2 — add 2πn to each

Two separate families, since sin has period \(2\pi\) and there are two particular solutions to build from.

Result

\(\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{6}+2\pi n\) or \(\theta = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}+2\pi n\), for any integer \(n\).

Worked example — tangent needs only one family

Find the general solution of tan θ = 1.

Step 1 — particular solutions on [0, 2π)

From Chapter 11: \(\theta=\pi/4\) or \(\theta=5\pi/4\) — but notice \(5\pi/4 = \pi/4+\pi\), exactly one tangent-period apart.

Step 2 — one family covers both

Since tangent repeats every \(\pi\) (equation 2.12.2), adding \(\pi n\) to \(\pi/4\) already sweeps out \(5\pi/4\) (at \(n=1\)) automatically — no second family needed.

Result

\(\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}+\pi n\), for any integer \(n\).

Common mistake

Using \(+2\pi n\) for a tangent equation out of habit, instead of \(+\pi n\). \(\theta=\pi/4+2\pi n\) is a real family of solutions — but it's only half of them, silently skipping \(5\pi/4, 5\pi/4+2\pi,\ldots\) entirely. Because tangent has the shorter period, its general solution is the one case in this module where the "+period" step uses \(\pi\), not \(2\pi\) — mixing this up produces an answer that's correct as far as it goes, but incomplete.

Try it yourself

Find the general solution of cos θ = 1. (Use k or n for the integer, e.g. 2pik.)

Practice

  1. Find the general solution of sin θ = √3/2.
    \(\theta=\pi/3+2\pi n\) or \(2\pi/3+2\pi n\)
  2. Find the general solution of cos θ = −1/2.
    \(\theta=2\pi/3+2\pi n\) or \(4\pi/3+2\pi n\)
  3. Find the general solution of tan θ = −1.
    \(\theta=3\pi/4+\pi n\)
  4. Find the general solution of sin θ = −1.
    \(\theta=3\pi/2+2\pi n\) (only one family — the "single-solution" peak/trough case)
  5. Find the general solution of tan θ = 0.
    \(\theta=\pi n\)
  6. List the first three positive solutions in the family θ = π/6 + 2πn.
    \(n=0: \pi/6\); \(n=1: \pi/6+2\pi=13\pi/6\); \(n=2: \pi/6+4\pi=25\pi/6\)
  7. bonus — Find the general solution of cos θ = √2/2.
    \(\theta=\pi/4+2\pi n\) or \(7\pi/4+2\pi n\) (equivalently \(-\pi/4+2\pi n\))
  8. bonus — Is θ = π/4 + 3π a member of the family θ = π/4 + πn? Which n?
    Yes, n = 3.
  9. bonus — Find the general solution of sin θ = 0, and explain why it needs only one family even though sine has period 2π (unlike tangent).
    \(\theta=\pi n\). Even though sin's period is \(2\pi\), its two particular solutions on one period (0 and π) are already exactly π apart, so a single \(+\pi n\) family happens to sweep out both — a coincidence of this specific equation, not a general rule for sin/cos.
  10. bonus — Find the general solution of tan θ = √3.
    \(\theta=\pi/3+\pi n\)
  11. challenge — A classmate writes the general solution of sin θ = 1/2 as a single family, θ = π/6 + πn, reasoning "π is a period too, from equation 2.6.1's logic applied twice." Plug in n=1 and check whether this actually lands on a true solution — then explain the flaw in their reasoning.
    n=1 gives θ=π/6+π=7π/6, and sin(7π/6)=-1/2 ≠ 1/2 — not a solution. The flaw: π is not a period of sin θ (only 2π is, per equation 2.6.1 with the "smallest such number" requirement) — sin(θ+π)=-sin(θ), a sign flip, not a repeat. Adding π moves to a different point on the circle where sine has the opposite sign, not the same value.
