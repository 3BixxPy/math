Tangent, Cotangent, Secant, Cosecant

Sine and cosine are the two primitive functions built directly from the unit circle. The other four trig functions aren't new ideas — every one of them is just a ratio built out of sine and cosine you already have.

Why this matters

It can feel like four more things to memorize, but there's nothing new here conceptually — these four functions are all defined in terms of sine and cosine. Get the definitions solid now and Chapters 9–10's identities (which relate all six functions to each other) become algebra you can redo, not facts you have to trust.

Definition — Tangent and cotangent
\[ \tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \cot\theta = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \frac{1}{\tan\theta} \]
(2.4.1)

Tangent is the ratio of the y-coordinate to the x-coordinate of the unit-circle point — geometrically, the slope of the terminal side. Cotangent is tangent flipped upside down.

Definition — Secant and cosecant
\[ \sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}, \qquad \csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta} \]
(2.4.2)

Secant and cosecant are simply the reciprocals of cosine and sine. Together with 2.4.1, this gives all six trig functions built from just two primitives.

Rule — Domain restrictions

You can't divide by zero, so wherever the denominator in a definition above hits zero, that function is undefined:

  • tan θ and sec θ are undefined wherever cos θ = 0 — that's \(\theta = \pi/2 + k\pi\) for any integer \(k\) (i.e. 90°, 270°, and every 180° from there).
  • cot θ and csc θ are undefined wherever sin θ = 0 — that's \(\theta = k\pi\) (0°, 180°, 360°, ...).

This is exactly why Chapter 8's graph of y = tan x has vertical asymptotes, not just a "steep" section.

Worked example

Find tan(60°) and sec(45°) using Chapter 3's exact values.

Step 1 — tan(60°): divide sin by cos

\(\tan60° = \dfrac{\sin60°}{\cos60°} = \dfrac{\sqrt3/2}{1/2} = \sqrt3\)

Step 2 — sec(45°): reciprocal of cos

\(\sec45° = \dfrac{1}{\cos45°} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt2/2} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt2} = \sqrt2\)

Worked example — cotangent and cosecant

Find cot(30°) and csc(60°).

Step 1 — cot(30°): divide cos by sin

\(\cot30° = \dfrac{\cos30°}{\sin30°} = \dfrac{\sqrt3/2}{1/2} = \sqrt3\)

Step 2 — csc(60°): reciprocal of sin

\(\csc60° = \dfrac{1}{\sin60°} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3/2} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt3} = \dfrac{2\sqrt3}{3}\)

Notice

\(\cot30° = \tan60°\) — both equal \(\sqrt3\). That's not a coincidence: 30° and 60° are complementary, and this pattern (cofunction of an angle equals the function of its complement) is a real identity you'll see used later, even though this course doesn't name it formally.

Common mistake

The naming trap: secant is the reciprocal of cosine, not sine — and cosecant is the reciprocal of sine, not cosine. It's backwards from what the "co-" prefix seems to suggest. Sine and cosine are cofunctions of each other, but "cosecant" does not mean "reciprocal of cosine" — it means "the co-function's reciprocal," and sine's cofunction is cosine only in the sense of complementary angles, not in the reciprocal pairing. The actual reciprocal pairs are: sin↔csc, cos↔sec, tan↔cot. When in doubt, don't guess from the name — go back to equation 2.4.2.

Practice

  1. Find sec(60°) exactly.
    \(\sec60°=1/\cos60°=1/(1/2)=2\)
  2. Find csc(30°) exactly.
    \(\csc30°=1/\sin30°=1/(1/2)=2\)
  3. Find cot(45°) exactly.
    \(\cot45°=\cos45°/\sin45°=1\)
  4. Find tan(30°) exactly.
    \(\tan30°=\sin30°/\cos30°=(1/2)/(\sqrt3/2)=1/\sqrt3=\sqrt3/3\)
  5. At which angle(s) in [0°, 360°) is tan θ undefined?
    90° and 270° (where cos θ = 0)
  6. At which angle(s) in [0°, 360°) is csc θ undefined?
    0° and 180° (where sin θ = 0)
  7. bonus — Find sec(120°) exactly. (Use Chapter 3's sign rules.)
    \(\cos120°=-1/2\), so \(\sec120°=1/(-1/2)=-2\)
  8. bonus — Find cot(90°).
    \(\cos90°/\sin90°=0/1=0\)
  9. bonus — Is it possible for sec θ to equal 0.5? Explain using the definition.
    No. \(\sec\theta=1/\cos\theta\), and \(|\cos\theta|\le1\) always, so \(|\sec\theta|\ge1\) always — it can never land strictly between -1 and 1.
  10. bonus — Find tan(135°) exactly.
    Reference angle 45°, QII: \(\sin135°=\sqrt2/2\), \(\cos135°=-\sqrt2/2\), so \(\tan135°=-1\)
  11. challenge — Explain, from the definitions in 2.4.1–2.4.2, why tan θ · cot θ = 1 for every θ where both are defined.
    \(\tan\theta\cdot\cot\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\cdot\dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \dfrac{\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\cos\theta\sin\theta}=1\) — direct algebra from the quotient definitions, with no new fact needed.
