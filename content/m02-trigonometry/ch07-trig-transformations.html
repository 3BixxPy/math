Chapter 7 · concept:trig-amplitude-period-phase

Transforming Sine & Cosine — Amplitude, Period, Phase Shift

Nothing new here structurally — this is Module 1's shift/stretch/reflection toolkit (Chapters 6–7 there) applied to sin and cos, plus one new wrinkle: stretching the x-axis changes the period, not just the shape's width.

Why this matters

Real waves — sound, light, tides, AC current, physics oscillations — are almost never plain sin x. They have their own height (amplitude), speed (period), starting point (phase shift), and baseline (vertical shift). This chapter is how you read all four off an equation, or build the equation from a description.

Theorem — General transformed sine
\[ y = A\sin\big(B(x-C)\big) + D \]
(2.7.1)
  • Amplitude \(=|A|\) — a vertical stretch/compression (Module 1, concept:vertical-stretch-compression), same rule as any function.
  • Period \(=\dfrac{2\pi}{|B|}\) — this one's new: stretching the x-axis by a factor doesn't just widen the graph, it changes how much x it takes to complete one cycle.
  • Phase shift \(=C\) — a horizontal shift (Module 1, concept:horizontal-shift), just given a wave-specific name.
  • Vertical shift \(=D\) — an ordinary vertical shift (Module 1, concept:vertical-shift).

Everything except the period formula is literally Module 1's transformation rules — reused, not re-derived.

Rule — Period formula
\[ T = \frac{2\pi}{|B|} \]
(2.7.2)

Why: the base wave sin x completes one cycle every time its input increases by \(2\pi\). In sin(Bx), the input is scaled by \(B\) — so \(x\) itself only needs to increase by \(2\pi/B\) for \(Bx\) to increase by a full \(2\pi\). Bigger \(B\) means the wave repeats faster (shorter period); \(B\) between 0 and 1 stretches the wave out (longer period).

Dashed horizontal lines mark the max/min (amplitude); the marked point sits at the phase-shifted, vertically-shifted "start" of the cycle.

Worked example

Graph y = 3sin(2x - π/2) + 1 by extracting all four parameters.

Step 1 — amplitude and vertical shift are direct

\(A=3\) (amplitude 3), \(D=1\) (vertical shift up 1) — read straight off the front and back of the expression.

Step 2 — period from B

\(B=2\), so period \(T = 2\pi/2 = \pi\) (equation 2.7.2).

Step 3 — phase shift: factor B out first

The equation is given as \(\sin(2x - \pi/2)\), not already in the \(\sin(B(x-C))\) form. Factor the 2 out of the whole inside expression: \(2x-\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 2\left(x-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\). So \(C=\pi/4\), not \(\pi/2\).

Result

Amplitude 3, period \(\pi\), phase shift \(\pi/4\) (right), vertical shift 1 (up). Max value \(3+1=4\), min value \(-3+1=-2\).

Common mistake

Reading the phase shift directly off the number next to \(x\) inside the parentheses, without dividing by \(B\) first. In sin(2x - π/2), it's tempting to say "phase shift = π/2" — but the theorem's form is \(\sin(B(x-C))\), a factored form, and \(2x-\pi/2\) is not yet factored. You must factor \(B\) out of the entire expression before \(C\) is whatever's left being subtracted from \(x\). Skipping this step is the single most common transformation error in this chapter.

Practice

  1. State the amplitude, period, phase shift, and vertical shift of y = 2sin(x - π) + 3.
    Amplitude 2, period \(2\pi\) (B=1), phase shift \(\pi\), vertical shift 3
  2. State the period of y = sin(3x).
    \(2\pi/3\)
  3. State the amplitude and vertical shift of y = -4cos(x) - 2.
    Amplitude 4, vertical shift -2 (down 2)
  4. Find the phase shift of y = sin(2x + π). (Careful — factor first.)
    \(2x+\pi = 2(x+\pi/2)\), so phase shift is \(-\pi/2\) (left \(\pi/2\)).
  5. Write an equation for a sine curve with amplitude 5, period π, no phase shift, vertical shift -1.
    Period \(\pi\) means \(B=2\pi/\pi=2\): \(y=5\sin(2x)-1\)
  6. What is the maximum value of y = 2sin(x) + 5?
    \(2+5=7\)
  7. bonus — State the period of y = cos(x/2).
    \(B=1/2\), period \(=2\pi/(1/2)=4\pi\)
  8. bonus — Find the phase shift of y = 4cos(3x - 3π).
    \(3x-3\pi=3(x-\pi)\), phase shift \(\pi\)
  9. bonus — Write an equation for a cosine curve with amplitude 1, period \(4\pi\), phase shift \(\pi\), vertical shift 0.
    \(B=2\pi/4\pi=1/2\): \(y=\cos\!\left(\dfrac12(x-\pi)\right)\)
  10. bonus — What is the minimum value of y = -3sin(x) + 2, and at what phase does it occur relative to plain sine?
    Minimum is \(-3+2=-1\). The negative A flips the wave, so the minimum happens where plain sin(x) would have its maximum.
  11. challenge — Two waves are given: y = sin(2x) and y = sin(2x - π). Find both periods and the phase shift between them, then explain why the second is just the first one reflected through the x-axis (same graph as y = -sin(2x)).
    Both have period \(2\pi/2=\pi\). Phase shift of the second: \(2x-\pi=2(x-\pi/2)\), so shift \(\pi/2\) — exactly half a period. Shifting a sine wave by half its own period flips it upside down, which is why \(\sin(2x-\pi) = -\sin(2x)\) identically.
