Module 2 Capstone — Mixed Review & Diagnostic Retake

Fourteen chapters, shuffled together, plus the trigonometry half of the original diagnostic — the part that came back as "one circle and something about sine waves, no concrete equations."

Why mixed, not grouped by chapter

Same reasoning as Module 0 and Module 1's capstones: a problem that doesn't announce its own chapter is the real test of whether the idea moved into durable memory, not just short-term pattern matching against a chapter title.

Mixed review (all of Module 2)

  1. Convert 300° to radians.
    \(5\pi/3\)
  2. Using the unit circle, find sin(π).
    \(0\)
  3. Find cos(135°) exactly.
    Reference angle 45°, QII cosine negative: \(-\sqrt2/2\)
  4. Find sec(30°) exactly.
    \(1/\cos30° = 1/(\sqrt3/2) = 2/\sqrt3 = 2\sqrt3/3\)
  5. A right triangle has an acute angle of 60° and hypotenuse 8. Find the opposite side.
    \(8\sin60° = 8(\sqrt3/2) = 4\sqrt3\)
  6. What is the period of y = cos x?
    \(2\pi\)
  7. State the amplitude and period of y = 4sin(2x) − 1.
    Amplitude 4, period \(2\pi/2=\pi\)
  8. What is the period of y = tan x?
    \(\pi\)
  9. If cos θ = 3/5 and θ is in QI, find sin θ.
    \(\sin^2\theta=1-9/25=16/25\); QI positive: \(4/5\)
  10. Simplify 1 + tan²x.
    \(\sec^2x\)
  11. Solve cos θ = −√3/2 on [0, 2π).
    \(\theta=5\pi/6\) or \(7\pi/6\)
  12. Find the general solution of sin θ = −1.
    \(\theta=3\pi/2+2\pi n\)
  13. Evaluate arccos(1/2).
    \(\pi/3\)
  14. Triangle with A = 45°, B = 60°, side a = 10. Find side b using the Law of Sines.
    \(b=10\sin60°/\sin45° \approx 12.25\)
  15. Find sin(300°) exactly.
    Reference angle 60°, QIV sine negative: \(-\sqrt3/2\)

Synthesis — concepts combining, not just shuffled

Two problems that each need multiple chapters' tools working together, not just one technique per problem.

Synthesis 1
Ch. 6 — graphingCh. 7 — transformationsCh. 8 — tangent contrast

For y = -2sin(2x - π) + 1: find the amplitude, period, phase shift, and vertical shift. Then explain why this same "amplitude" analysis would not directly apply to y = 3tan(2x).

Step 1 — factor the inside expression to find the phase shift

\(2x-\pi = 2(x-\pi/2)\), so in the form \(A\sin(B(x-C))+D\): \(A=-2\), \(B=2\), \(C=\pi/2\), \(D=1\).

Step 2 — read off amplitude, period, vertical shift

Amplitude \(=|-2|=2\); period \(=2\pi/2=\pi\) (equation 2.7.2); vertical shift \(=1\). The negative A also means the curve is flipped — where plain sine would peak, this one dips.

Step 3 — why tangent breaks the analogy

y=3tan(2x) has a period too — \(\pi/|B|=\pi/2\) (Chapter 8's halved period formula, not sine's \(2\pi/|B|\)) — but "amplitude" has no meaning for it. Tangent is unbounded (Chapter 8); the 3 stretches how steeply it climbs toward each asymptote, but there's no maximum height to call an amplitude, and no dashed max/min reference lines the way a sine curve has.

Result

Sine curve: amplitude 2, period π, phase shift π/2, vertical shift 1, reflected. Tangent's period formula transfers (with its own π-based rule), but amplitude does not — it's a concept that only exists for bounded functions.

Synthesis 2
Ch. 9 — Pythagorean identityCh. 10 — identity manipulationCh. 11 — solving equations

Solve 2cos²θ + sinθ - 1 = 0 on [0, 2π).

Step 1 — the equation mixes sin and cos; use the Pythagorean identity to unify it (Chapter 9/10's technique)

\(\cos^2\theta = 1-\sin^2\theta\) (equation 2.9.1, rearranged). Substitute: \(2(1-\sin^2\theta)+\sin\theta-1=0\).

Step 2 — simplify into a quadratic in sin θ

\(2-2\sin^2\theta+\sin\theta-1=0 \Rightarrow -2\sin^2\theta+\sin\theta+1=0 \Rightarrow 2\sin^2\theta-\sin\theta-1=0\) (multiplied by -1).

Step 3 — factor, same as any quadratic (Module 0)

\((2\sin\theta+1)(\sin\theta-1)=0 \Rightarrow \sin\theta=-\dfrac12\) or \(\sin\theta=1\).

Step 4 — solve each on [0, 2π) using Chapter 11's method

\(\sin\theta=-1/2\): reference angle \(\pi/6\), sine negative in QIII/QIV → \(\theta=7\pi/6, 11\pi/6\). \(\sin\theta=1\): the single-solution peak case (Chapter 11's mistake note) → \(\theta=\pi/2\).

Result

\(\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{7\pi}{6}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6}\) — three solutions, found by chaining an identity substitution (Ch. 9), quadratic factoring, and restricted-domain solving (Ch. 11) into one problem.

Diagnostic retake

The trigonometry section of the original diagnostic came back with "one circle and something about sine waves — no concrete equations." Here it is again, now with fourteen chapters of concrete equations behind it.

1. What is sin(30°)? Give an exact value, not a decimal.

Originally: vague — no concrete answer offered.

\(1/2\) — exact, from the 30-60-90 triangle. See Chapter 3.

2. Describe the graph of y = sin x over one period. What are its amplitude and period?

Originally: "something about a wave" — no numbers attached.

Amplitude 1, period \(2\pi\); starts at 0, rises to a max of 1 at \(\pi/2\), back to 0 at \(\pi\), down to a min of -1 at \(3\pi/2\), back to 0 at \(2\pi\). See Chapter 6.

3. What is sin²θ + cos²θ?

Originally: unknown — no identity had ever been introduced.

\(1\), for every real θ — the Pythagorean identity, and the most-reused fact in the whole module. See Chapter 9.
