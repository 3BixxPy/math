Increasing/Decreasing & Even/Odd Functions

Two ways of describing a graph's behavior without needing to see the whole picture — both come back explicitly once derivatives and trig functions arrive.

Why this matters

"Where is this function increasing?" is precisely the question a derivative's sign answers in Calculus I (Module 7, curve sketching) — this chapter is the plain-language version before there's a formula for it. Even/odd symmetry, meanwhile, is exactly the property that makes sine an odd function and cosine an even one in Module 2 — recognizing it here means it won't be a new idea then, just a new example.

Definition — Increasing / decreasing

A function is increasing on an interval if, as x increases through that interval, y also increases (the graph rises left to right). It's decreasing if y falls as x increases (the graph drops left to right).

y=(x-2)²-1: falling (decreasing) until the vertex at x=2, then rising (increasing) after.

Definition — Even and odd functions
\[ \text{Even: } f(-x) = f(x) \qquad\qquad \text{Odd: } f(-x) = -f(x) \]
(1.12.1)

An even function looks identical when reflected over the y-axis (plug in −x, get the same output back). An odd function looks the same after a 180° rotation about the origin (plug in −x, get the exact negative of the original output). Most functions are neither.

y = x² (even)y = x³ (odd)

x² is a mirror image of itself across the y-axis. x³ looks the same after spinning it 180° around the origin.

Worked example — testing algebraically

Is f(x) = x² + x even, odd, or neither?

Step 1 — compute f(-x)

\(f(-x) = (-x)^2+(-x) = x^2-x\)

Step 2 — check the even condition: does f(-x) = f(x)?

\(x^2-x \neq x^2+x\) (unless x=0) — not even.

Step 3 — check the odd condition: does f(-x) = -f(x)?

\(-f(x) = -x^2-x\). Compare to \(f(-x)=x^2-x\): not equal — not odd either.

Result

f(x)=x²+x is neither even nor odd.

Common mistake

Assuming a function is "even" just because it contains a term with an even exponent. x²+x has an \(x^2\) term but is not an even function — the plain \(x\) term breaks the symmetry, as the worked example just showed. "Even function" describes the whole function's symmetry, checked with the actual definition, not a shortcut based on skimming the exponents.

Try it yourself

Is f(x) = x⁵ even, odd, or neither? (type one word)

Practice

  1. Is f(x) = x⁴ even, odd, or neither?
    Even — \(f(-x)=x^4=f(x)\)
  2. Is f(x) = x⁵ even, odd, or neither?
    Odd — \(f(-x)=-x^5=-f(x)\)
  3. Is f(x) = x² - 3 even, odd, or neither?
    Even
  4. Is f(x) = 2x + 1 even, odd, or neither?
    Neither
  5. For f(x) = (x-2)² - 1, on what interval is f decreasing?
    \(x < 2\)
  6. For the same f, on what interval is it increasing?
    \(x > 2\)
  7. bonus — Is f(x) = |x| even, odd, or neither?
    Even — \(|{-x}|=|x|\)
  8. bonus — Is f(x) = x³ - x even, odd, or neither?
    Odd — \(f(-x)=-x^3+x=-(x^3-x)=-f(x)\)
  9. bonus — Is the zero function, f(x)=0, even, odd, both, or neither? (trick question)
    Both — \(f(-x)=0=f(x)\) (even) and \(f(-x)=0=-f(x)\) (odd) are simultaneously true, since \(0=-0\). It's the one function where both hold.
  10. bonus — A graph is symmetric about the origin and passes through (3, 5). What other point must also be on it?
    \((-3,-5)\)
  11. challenge — Prove algebraically that the sum of two odd functions is always odd: if f and g are both odd, show h(x) = f(x)+g(x) is odd too.
    \(h(-x)=f(-x)+g(-x) = -f(x)+(-g(x)) = -(f(x)+g(x)) = -h(x)\), which is exactly the odd condition.
