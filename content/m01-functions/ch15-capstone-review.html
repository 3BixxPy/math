Module 1 Capstone — Mixed Review & Diagnostic Retake

Fourteen chapters, shuffled together, plus the functions/graphs half of the very first diagnostic — the part that originally came back "I don't know anything."

Why mixed, not grouped by chapter

Same reasoning as Module 0's capstone: a problem that doesn't announce its own chapter is the real test of whether the idea moved into durable memory, not just short-term pattern matching against a chapter title.

Mixed review (all of Module 1)

  1. Is {(1,4), (2,4), (1,7)} a function?
    No — input 1 has two different outputs.
  2. If f(x) = 3x² − 1, find f(-3).
    \(3(9)-1=26\)
  3. Find the domain: 1/(x+6)
    \(x\neq-6\)
  4. Which quadrant is (-4, -9) in?
    Quadrant III
  5. Find the slope between (0, 5) and (3, -1).
    \(-6/3=-2\)
  6. Describe how y = (x+4)² − 2 relates to y = x².
    Shifted left 4, down 2.
  7. Is y = -3x² a stretch or compression of y = x², and is it reflected?
    Stretch (|−3|>1), and reflected over the x-axis.
  8. Find the vertex of y = x² − 10x + 21.
    \(h=5, k=-4\), vertex \((5,-4)\)
  9. For f(x) = {x-1 if x<1; 3 if x≥1}, find f(1).
    3 (uses the ≥1 piece)
  10. If f(x) = x+4 and g(x) = x², find g(f(1)).
    \(f(1)=5\), \(g(5)=25\)
  11. Find the inverse of f(x) = 5x − 2.
    \(f^{-1}(x)=(x+2)/5\)
  12. Is f(x) = x⁴ − 2x² even, odd, or neither?
    Even
  13. Describe the end behavior of f(x) = -4x⁵ + x.
    Odd degree, negative leading coefficient: left end up, right end down.
  14. Find the vertical and horizontal asymptotes of f(x) = 3x/(x-1).
    VA \(x=1\); HA \(y=3\) (equal degrees, leading coefficients 3 and 1)

Diagnostic retake

The functions/graphs section of the original diagnostic came back with no attempts — completely blank. Here it is again.

1. If f(x) = 2x² − 3, find f(-2).

Originally: no attempt.

\(f(-2) = 2(4)-3 = 5\). See Chapter 2.

2. What is the domain of 1/(x-4)? Of √(x-4)?

Originally: no attempt.

\(1/(x-4)\): \(x\neq4\). \(\sqrt{x-4}\): \(x\geq4\). See Chapter 3.

3. How does the graph of y = (x-2)² + 1 relate to y = x²?

Originally: no attempt.

Shifted right 2, up 1 — vertex moves from (0,0) to (2,1). See Chapter 6.
