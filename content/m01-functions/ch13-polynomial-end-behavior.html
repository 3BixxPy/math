Polynomial Functions & End Behavior

What happens to a graph way off to the left and way off to the right — decided by exactly two numbers, without needing to plot anything.

Why this matters

"What does y do as x→∞?" is a question you'll ask constantly starting in Calculus I, formalized there as a limit at infinity. This chapter answers it for polynomials with a simple rule, before "limit" is even a defined word — so when it is, this will already feel familiar.

Definition — Degree and leading coefficient
\[ a_n x^n + \cdots + a_1 x + a_0 \]
(1.13.1)

The degree is the highest power of x present (\(n\)). The leading coefficient is the number multiplying that highest power (\(a_n\)). Together, these two numbers alone decide what the graph does at its far left and far right ends.

Rule — Four end-behavior cases
  • Even degree, positive leading coefficient: both ends go up (like x²)
  • Even degree, negative leading coefficient: both ends go down (like -x²)
  • Odd degree, positive leading coefficient: left end down, right end up (like x³)
  • Odd degree, negative leading coefficient: left end up, right end down (like -x³)

Only the leading term matters for this — every other term becomes insignificant compared to it once |x| is large enough.

Worked example

Describe the end behavior of f(x) = -2x³ + 5x - 1.

Step 1 — identify degree and leading coefficient

Degree 3 (odd), leading coefficient \(-2\) (negative).

Step 2 — apply the rule (odd, negative)

Left end up, right end down.

Result

As \(x\to-\infty\), \(y\to+\infty\); as \(x\to+\infty\), \(y\to-\infty\). The \(5x-1\) part never gets a say — for huge \(|x|\), \(-2x^3\) completely dominates.

Common mistake

Judging end behavior from the sign of the leading coefficient alone, ignoring whether the degree is even or odd. A negative leading coefficient means "both ends down" only if the degree is even; with an odd degree, a negative leading coefficient actually flips the two ends in opposite directions, not the same one. Always check both numbers.

Try it yourself

What's the end behavior of f(x) = 3x⁴ - 2x² + 1? (type e.g. bothup, bothdown, leftdownrightup, or leftuprightdown)

Practice

  1. State the degree and leading coefficient: f(x) = 3x⁴ - 2x² + 1
    Degree 4, leading coefficient 3
  2. Describe the end behavior of that same f(x).
    Both ends up (even, positive)
  3. State the degree and leading coefficient: f(x) = -5x³ + x - 2
    Degree 3, leading coefficient -5
  4. Describe the end behavior of that same f(x).
    Left end up, right end down (odd, negative)
  5. Describe the end behavior of f(x) = -x⁶ + 4x.
    Both ends down (even, negative)
  6. Describe the end behavior of f(x) = 2x⁵ - 3x².
    Left end down, right end up (odd, positive)
  7. bonus — What is the maximum number of direction changes a degree-5 polynomial's graph can have?
    4 (degree − 1)
  8. bonus — A polynomial has odd degree and positive leading coefficient. What happens to y as x→−∞?
    y→−∞ (left end down)
  9. bonus — Two different polynomials both have "both ends up" behavior. What must be true about each one's degree and leading coefficient sign?
    Both must have even degree and a positive leading coefficient.
  10. bonus — Explain in one sentence why only the leading term affects end behavior.
    For very large |x|, the highest power grows so much faster than the lower-degree terms that they become negligible in comparison.
  11. challenge — Without expanding fully, determine the end behavior of f(x) = -(x-1)²(x+3)³ by figuring out its overall degree and the sign of its leading term.
    Degree = 2+3 = 5 (odd). Leading term comes from \(x^2\cdot x^3=x^5\), times the outer \(-1\): \(-x^5\), so leading coefficient is negative. Odd + negative → left end up, right end down.
