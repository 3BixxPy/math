Transformations I — Shifts

The diagnostic asked, roughly, how y=(x−2)²+1 relates to y=x². This chapter answers that exactly — and lets you drag it and watch.

Why this matters

Once you know how +k and -h move a graph, you can sketch any transformed version of a shape you already know — a parabola, later a sine wave, later still an exponential — without plotting a single new point. It's the reductionist move applied to graphing: one base shape, reused everywhere.

Rule — Vertical shift
\[ y = f(x) + k \]
(1.6.1)

Adding k outside the function moves the whole graph up (k > 0) or down (k < 0) by k units. This one works exactly the way it looks.

Rule — Horizontal shift
\[ y = f(x - h) \]
(1.6.2)

Subtracting h inside the function moves the graph right by h units (for h > 0) — the opposite of what the minus sign suggests. f(x+3) shifts left 3.

Common mistake — the sign flip nobody expects

y = f(x-2) shifts right, not left, even though it's "minus 2." Think about why: the new graph reaches its old value of \(f(0)\) when \(x-2=0\), i.e. when \(x=2\) — two steps to the right of where it used to happen. Whatever used to occur at x=0 now occurs at x=h. The inside of the function moves opposite to what the sign looks like; the outside (the +k) moves exactly as it looks.

Worked example — the exact diagnostic question

How does y = (x-2)² + 1 relate to y = x²?

Step 1 — identify h and k by comparing to y = (x-h)² + k

\(h = 2\), \(k = 1\)

Step 2 — apply the horizontal shift rule

\((x-2)\) inside → shift right 2

Step 3 — apply the vertical shift rule

\(+1\) outside → shift up 1

Result

The graph of y=(x-2)²+1 is the graph of y=x², moved right 2 and up 1 — same shape, new location. Its lowest point (the vertex) moves from \((0,0)\) to \((2,1)\).

y = (x−2)² + 1 — vertex at (2, 1)

Practice

  1. Describe how y = x² + 5 relates to y = x².
    Shifted up 5.
  2. Describe how y = (x-3)² relates to y = x².
    Shifted right 3.
  3. Describe how y = (x+1)² - 2 relates to y = x².
    Shifted left 1, down 2.
  4. What is the vertex of y = (x-5)² + 3?
    \((5, 3)\)
  5. What is the vertex of y = (x+2)² - 7?
    \((-2, -7)\)
  6. If a graph's lowest point moves from \((0,0)\) to \((-4, 6)\), what are h and k in \(y=(x-h)^2+k\)?
    \(h=-4, k=6\)
  7. bonus — Describe how y = √(x-4) + 2 relates to y = √x (the shift rules apply to any function, not just parabolas).
    Shifted right 4, up 2.
  8. bonus — Write the equation of y = x² shifted left 6 and down 1.
    \(y=(x+6)^2-1\)
  9. bonus — Two students disagree: one says y=(x-2)² shifts left, the other says right. Settle it by checking a specific value — evaluate both graphs at their own vertex and explain.
    The vertex of \(y=(x-2)^2\) is where \(x-2=0\), i.e. \(x=2\) — to the right of the original vertex at \(x=0\). So: right.
  10. bonus — Does the shape of a parabola change under a shift? What changes and what doesn't?
    The shape (width, whether it opens up or down) stays identical — only its position moves.
  11. challenge — A parabola \(y=x^2\) is shifted so its vertex lands exactly on the point where the line \(y=2x-1\) crosses the y-axis, and also shifted an extra 3 units to the right of that. Find the resulting equation.
    The line's y-intercept is \((0,-1)\); shifting 3 more right puts the vertex at \((3,-1)\): \(y=(x-3)^2-1\).
