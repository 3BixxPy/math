Chapter 10 · concept:lhopitals-rule

L'Hôpital's Rule

Module 5's entire indeterminate-forms unit had exactly one general tool: factor-and-cancel (Ch. 5), plus the squeeze theorem for the trig limits it couldn't touch (Ch. 10–11). Now that derivatives exist, there's a second, far more powerful tool — and it's satisfying precisely because you already know what answer to expect.

Why this matters

Factoring only works when the indeterminate expression happens to factor nicely — plenty of 0/0 and ∞/∞ limits don't. L'Hôpital's Rule handles the indeterminate form directly, using derivatives instead of algebraic tricks, and it works on limits factoring can't touch at all (like ones involving eˣ, ln x, or trig functions tangled together).

Theorem — L'Hôpital's Rule

If lim f(x)/g(x) as x→a (or x→±∞) is an indeterminate form 0/0 or ∞/∞, and lim f′(x)/g′(x) exists, then:

\[ \lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)} \]
(7.10.1)

Differentiate the numerator and denominator separately — this is not the quotient rule, and the result is a brand-new fraction, not a derivative of the original one. If that new limit is still indeterminate, the rule can be applied again, as many times as needed.

Worked example — sin(x)/x, two different ways

Evaluate limx→0 sin(x)/x using L'Hôpital's Rule, and compare to Module 5's squeeze-theorem answer.

Step 1 — confirm the indeterminate form

Direct substitution: \(\sin(0)/0 = 0/0\) — indeterminate, (7.10.1) applies.

Step 2 — differentiate numerator and denominator separately

\(\dfrac{d}{dx}[\sin x] = \cos x\), \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[x]=1\).

Step 3 — evaluate the new limit

\(\lim_{x\to0}\dfrac{\cos x}{1} = \cos(0) = 1\)

Result

Same answer as Module 5, Chapter 11's squeeze-theorem argument: \(1\) — confirmed by a completely different method.

Worked example — an ∞/∞ form

Evaluate limx→∞ ln(x)/x.

Step 1 — confirm the indeterminate form

As \(x\to\infty\), both \(\ln x\to\infty\) and \(x\to\infty\): an ∞/∞ form.

Step 2 — differentiate top and bottom

\(\dfrac{d}{dx}[\ln x]=\dfrac1x\), \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[x]=1\).

Result

\(\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{1/x}{1} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac1x = 0\) — logarithms lose to any positive power of \(x\), no matter how slowly \(x\) grows.

Common mistake

Applying L'Hôpital's Rule to a limit that isn't actually an indeterminate 0/0 or ∞/∞ form. For limx→2 (x+3)/(x-1), direct substitution gives 5/1 = 5 — a perfectly good, non-indeterminate answer. Applying L'Hôpital's Rule anyway (differentiating to get 1/1 = 1) gives a wrong answer, \(1\neq5\). The rule is only valid on a genuine indeterminate form; checking the form with direct substitution first isn't an optional formality, it's the condition that makes the whole theorem apply at all.

Try it yourself

Evaluate limx→0 (eˣ − 1)/x using L'Hôpital's Rule.

Practice

  1. Evaluate limx→0 (1 − cos x)/x.
    0/0 form. \(\lim \sin(x)/1 = 0\)
  2. Evaluate limx→∞ (5x − 2)/(3x + 7).
    ∞/∞ form. \(\lim 5/3 = 5/3\)
  3. Evaluate limx→1 (x² − 1)/(x − 1) using L'Hôpital's Rule.
    0/0 form. \(\lim 2x/1 = 2\) (matches Module 5, Ch. 5's factor-and-cancel answer)
  4. Evaluate limx→0 (e^{2x} − 1)/(3x).
    0/0 form. \(\lim 2e^{2x}/3 = 2/3\)
  5. Is limx→3 (x² − 9)/(x + 3) an indeterminate form? Evaluate it.
    No — direct substitution gives \(0/6=0\), which is a perfectly valid, non-indeterminate answer. L'Hôpital's Rule isn't needed (or valid) here.
  6. Evaluate limx→∞ x/eˣ.
    ∞/∞ form. \(\lim 1/e^x = 0\)
  7. bonus — Evaluate limx→0 tan(x)/x.
    0/0 form. \(\lim \sec^2(x)/1 = \sec^2(0)=1\)
  8. bonus — Evaluate limx→∞ (x² + 1)/(2x² − x) using L'Hôpital's Rule (applied twice if needed).
    ∞/∞ form. First application: \(2x/(4x-1)\), still ∞/∞. Second: \(2/4=1/2\) (matches Module 5, Ch. 6's equal-degree rule)
  9. bonus — Evaluate limx→0 (x − sin x)/x³ (0/0, apply L'Hôpital's Rule three times).
    \((1-\cos x)/(3x^2)\) [0/0] \(\to \sin(x)/(6x)\) [0/0] \(\to \cos(x)/6 \to 1/6\)
  10. bonus — Explain in words why limx→0 x·cos(1/x) is NOT something L'Hôpital's Rule could help with.
    It's a product, not a quotient in 0/0 or ∞/∞ form — L'Hôpital's Rule doesn't apply to products at all. (It's also unnecessary here: \(\cos(1/x)\) is bounded between \(-1\) and \(1\), and \(x\to0\), so the squeeze theorem from Module 5, Ch. 10 settles it directly — the limit is \(0\).)
  11. challenge — Evaluate limx→0 (eˣ − 1 − x)/x² (0/0, apply L'Hôpital's Rule twice).
    First application: \((e^x-1)/(2x)\), still 0/0. Second application: \(e^x/2 \to 1/2\).
