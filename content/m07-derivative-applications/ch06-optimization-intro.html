Optimization — Setting Up the Problem

Everything so far — critical points, the two classification tests — has been aimed at functions someone already handed you. This chapter is where you build the function yourself, out of a word problem, and then let Chapters 2–4 do the rest.

Why this matters

"What's the best value of x" only makes sense once there's a single function of x to ask it about. Real problems rarely arrive that way — they arrive as a sentence with two related quantities and a constraint tying them together. The actual calculus (finding and classifying a critical point) is the easy, mechanical part by this point in Module 7; translating the word problem into one clean function of one variable is the genuinely new skill.

Method — the optimization strategy
  1. Identify the objective — the single quantity to maximize or minimize.
  2. Identify the constraint — the fixed relationship between the variables (often given directly, like a fixed perimeter).
  3. Use the constraint to write the objective as a function of one variable (substitution — Module 0).
  4. Find the critical point(s) of that one-variable function (Chapter 2).
  5. Classify each as an actual max or min (Chapter 3 or 4).
  6. Check that the answer actually falls within the problem's physically valid domain — a negative length or an over-large angle is nonsense, even if it's a critical point of the unrestricted function.
Worked example — the fixed-perimeter rectangle

A rectangle has a perimeter of 20. What dimensions maximize its area?

Step 1 — identify the objective and the constraint

Objective: maximize area \(A=xy\). Constraint: perimeter is fixed, \(2x+2y=20\), i.e. \(x+y=10\).

Step 2 — substitute to reduce to one variable

From the constraint, \(y=10-x\). Substitute:

\[ A(x) = x(10-x) = 10x - x^2 \]
(7.6.1)
Step 3 — find the critical point

\(A'(x)=10-2x=0 \Rightarrow x=5\).

Step 4 — classify it

\(A''(x)=-2<0\) → local max, by the Second Derivative Test (Ch. 4).

Result

\(x=5\), \(y=10-5=5\) — a \(5\times5\) square, with maximum area \(A(5)=25\).

Common mistake

Forgetting Step 6 — never checking whether the critical point actually falls inside the problem's physically valid domain. The unrestricted function A(x)=10x-x² is a downward parabola defined for every real x, including negative ones, but a rectangle can't have a negative side length. In this example the critical point happened to land safely inside \((0,10)\), but that's not guaranteed in general — always state the valid domain from the constraint and confirm the critical point is inside it before declaring it the final answer.

Practice

  1. A rectangle has perimeter 40. What dimensions maximize its area?
    \(A(x)=x(20-x)\), \(A'=20-2x=0\Rightarrow x=10\), a \(10\times10\) square, area \(100\)
  2. A rectangle has a fixed area of 100. What dimensions minimize its perimeter?
    \(P(x)=2x+2(100/x)=2x+200/x\), \(P'(x)=2-200/x^2=0\Rightarrow x^2=100\Rightarrow x=10\). Again a \(10\times10\) square, perimeter \(40\) — the dual of a maximum-area problem is a minimum-perimeter one.
  3. Two positive numbers sum to 20. What values maximize their product?
    \(P(x)=x(20-x)\), \(P'=20-2x=0\Rightarrow x=10\), so both numbers are \(10\), product \(100\)
  4. Two positive numbers sum to 16. What values minimize the sum of their squares?
    \(S(x)=x^2+(16-x)^2\), \(S'=2x-2(16-x)=4x-32=0\Rightarrow x=8\), so both numbers are \(8\), minimum sum of squares \(128\)
  5. A rectangular field needs fencing on three sides only (the fourth runs along a river), with 100 ft of fence total. Maximize the enclosed area.
    Let \(x\) = each of the two sides perpendicular to the river, \(y\) = the side parallel to it: \(2x+y=100\Rightarrow y=100-2x\). \(A(x)=x(100-2x)=100x-2x^2\), \(A'=100-4x=0\Rightarrow x=25\), \(y=50\). Max area \(1250\) sq ft.
  6. A rectangle has perimeter 24. What dimensions maximize its area?
    \(x=6\), a \(6\times6\) square, area \(36\)
  7. bonus — Two positive numbers sum to 30. What values minimize the sum of their squares?
    Both equal \(15\), by the same setup as problem 4
  8. bonus — A pen against a barn wall (no fence needed on that side) uses 80 ft of fence for the other three sides. Maximize the enclosed area.
    \(2x+y=80\Rightarrow y=80-2x\). \(A(x)=x(80-2x)=80x-2x^2\), \(A'=80-4x=0\Rightarrow x=20\), \(y=40\). Max area \(800\) sq ft.
  9. bonus — Two positive numbers have a product of 100. What values minimize their sum?
    \(S(x)=x+100/x\), \(S'=1-100/x^2=0\Rightarrow x=10\), so both numbers are \(10\), minimum sum \(20\)
  10. bonus — The objective function A(x) = 30x − x² has already been set up, with valid domain 0. Find the x that maximizes it.
    \(A'(x)=30-2x=0\Rightarrow x=15\), safely inside the domain
  11. challenge — For a rectangle with a general fixed perimeter P (a symbolic constant, not a specific number), show that a square always maximizes the area.
    \(x+y=P/2 \Rightarrow y=P/2-x\). \(A(x)=x(P/2-x)=\tfrac{P}{2}x-x^2\). \(A'(x)=\tfrac{P}{2}-2x=0\Rightarrow x=P/4\), and then \(y=P/2-P/4=P/4=x\) — always equal, regardless of the value of \(P\). The numeric pattern from every problem above (5&5, 10&10, 20&20...) wasn't a coincidence; it's this general result.
