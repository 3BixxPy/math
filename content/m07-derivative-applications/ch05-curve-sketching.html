Chapter 5 · concept:curve-sketching

Curve Sketching — Putting It All Together

Chapters 2 through 4 built three separate tools: where the interesting x-values are, whether each is a peak or valley, and how the curve bends between them. This chapter doesn't add a new tool — it's the payoff of combining the three you already have into an actually trustworthy sketch.

Why this matters

Plotting a handful of points and connecting the dots is guesswork disguised as a graph — it can easily miss a local max/min sitting between two sampled x-values, or get the concavity backwards. A sketch built from derivative-driven features instead is exact, everywhere, because it's built from what the function is actually doing, not from what a few lucky (or unlucky) sample points happened to show.

Method — the curve-sketching checklist
  1. Domain (Module 1) — where is f even defined?
  2. Intercepts — the y-intercept f(0), and x-intercepts where f(x)=0
  3. Asymptotes / end behavior (Module 1, Ch. 13–14) — what happens as x→±∞, and any vertical asymptotes
  4. Increasing / decreasing intervals — the sign of f′ (Chapter 3)
  5. Concavity and inflection points — the sign of f″ (Chapter 4)
  6. Assemble — plot the key points (intercepts, critical points, inflection points) and connect them consistently with steps 4–5

No new formula here — this chapter is entirely about running the checklist in order and trusting it over intuition.

Worked example — sketching f(x)=x³−3x² feature by feature

This is the same function Chapters 2–4 have been building up analysis of — now assembled into one full picture.

Step 1 — domain

A polynomial: defined for all real \(x\).

Step 2 — intercepts

y-intercept: \(f(0)=0\). x-intercepts: \(x^3-3x^2=x^2(x-3)=0 \Rightarrow x=0,3\). So \((0,0)\) and \((3,0)\).

Step 3 — end behavior

Odd degree, positive leading coefficient (Module 1, Ch. 13): as \(x\to-\infty\), \(f\to-\infty\); as \(x\to+\infty\), \(f\to+\infty\). No asymptotes — it's a polynomial.

Step 4 — increasing / decreasing (from Chapter 3)

\(f'(x)=3x^2-6x\): increasing on \((-\infty,0)\), decreasing on \((0,2)\), increasing on \((2,\infty)\). Local max at \((0,0)\), local min at \((2,-4)\).

Step 5 — concavity (from Chapter 4)

\(f''(x)=6x-6\): concave down on \((-\infty,1)\), concave up on \((1,\infty)\). Inflection point at \((1,-2)\).

Result — assembling the sketch

Starting from the far left (down and to the left), the curve rises, caving downward, up through \((0,0)\) — a local max where it turns around. It falls, still caving downward until \((1,-2)\), where it switches to caving upward, continuing to fall to the local min at \((2,-4)\). From there it rises again, now caving upward, crossing \((3,0)\) and continuing up and to the right forever. Every feature is accounted for — nothing was guessed.

local max / min inflection point

Check your own hand-sketch of f(x)=x³−3x² against this — every marked point came from Chapters 3–4, not from plugging in random x-values.

Common mistake

Sketching from a handful of plugged-in points instead of from the derivative-driven features. For f(x)=x³−3x², the true local max, local min, and inflection point sit at x=0, 2, and 1 — but in general those special x-values can just as easily land on a fraction or an irrational number, nowhere near the "nice" integers a naive sketch would sample. Connecting a few evenly-spaced plotted points can easily miss a hidden bump between two of them, or draw the wrong concavity across a stretch where it actually switches. The checklist is slower point-for-point, but it's exact everywhere — a few plugged-in points are just a guess that happens to look plausible.

Practice

  1. Run the checklist on f(x) = x² − 4x + 3: intercepts, critical point, concavity.
    Intercepts \((1,0),(3,0),(0,3)\). \(f'=2x-4=0\Rightarrow x=2\) (min, \(y=-1\)). \(f''=2>0\): concave up everywhere, no inflection. Standard upward parabola.
  2. Run the checklist on f(x) = -x³ + 3x: critical points, concavity, inflection point.
    \(f'=-3x^2+3=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\). \(f''=-6x\): \(f''(1)=-6<0\) (local max, \(y=2\)); \(f''(-1)=6>0\) (local min, \(y=-2\)). Inflection at \(x=0\) (sign of \(f''\) switches there), \(f(0)=0\).
  3. Run the checklist on f(x) = x⁴ − 2x²: critical points and inflection points.
    \(f'=4x^3-4x=4x(x-1)(x+1)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\pm1\). Local max at \(x=0\), local min at \(x=\pm1\) (even function, symmetric). \(f''=12x^2-4=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1/\sqrt3\): both inflection points.
  4. Run the checklist on f(x) = 1/(x-1): asymptotes, increasing/decreasing, concavity.
    Vertical asymptote \(x=1\), horizontal asymptote \(y=0\) (Module 1, Ch. 14). \(f'=-1/(x-1)^2<0\) always → decreasing on both branches. \(f''=2/(x-1)^3\): negative for \(x<1\) (concave down), positive for \(x>1\) (concave up) — but not an inflection point, since \(f\) isn't even defined at \(x=1\).
  5. Run the checklist on f(x) = x³ − 3x² − 9x: critical points and inflection point.
    \(f'=3x^2-6x-9=3(x-3)(x+1)=0\Rightarrow x=-1,3\). \(f(-1)=5\) (local max), \(f(3)=-27\) (local min). \(f''=6x-6=0\Rightarrow x=1\), \(f(1)=-11\): inflection point.
  6. Run the checklist on f(x) = x^{1/3}: domain, critical points, monotonicity.
    Domain: all reals. Critical point at \(x=0\) (derivative undefined there, Ch. 2), but no sign change in \(f'\) (Ch. 3) — the function is increasing everywhere, just with a vertical tangent at the origin.
  7. bonus — Run the checklist on f(x) = x⁴ − 4x² + 3: critical points, classification, inflection points.
    \(f'=4x^3-8x=4x(x^2-2)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\pm\sqrt2\). \(f''=12x^2-8\): \(f''(0)=-8<0\) (local max); \(f''(\pm\sqrt2)=16>0\) (local min, both). Inflection at \(x=\pm\sqrt{2/3}\).
  8. bonus — Run the checklist on f(x) = x/(x²+1): critical points, asymptotes.
    Quotient rule: \(f'=(1-x^2)/(x^2+1)^2=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1\). \(f(1)=1/2\) (local max), \(f(-1)=-1/2\) (local min). Horizontal asymptote \(y=0\); no vertical asymptote (denominator never \(0\)).
  9. bonus — Given only that f' is negative on (−∞,−2), positive on (−2,1), negative on (1,∞), and f'' is positive on (−∞,−0.5), negative on (−0.5,∞) — describe the shape: critical points, classification, and inflection point.
    \(x=-2\): local min (\(-\to+\)). \(x=1\): local max (\(+\to-\)). Inflection point at \(x=-0.5\) (concavity switches up to down). No formula needed — the checklist reads straight off the sign information.
  10. bonus — Run the checklist on f(x) = 3x⁵ − 5x³: find and classify the critical points.
    \(f'=15x^4-15x^2=15x^2(x^2-1)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\pm1\). Sign of \(f'\) (matches sign of \((x^2-1)\) except at \(x=0\)): \(+\) on \((-\infty,-1)\), \(-\) on \((-1,1)\) (including across \(x=0\), no sign change there), \(+\) on \((1,\infty)\). \(x=-1\): local max. \(x=0\): neither. \(x=1\): local min.
  11. challenge — Fully sketch f(x) = x⁴ − 4x³ + 4x² = x²(x-2)² using the complete checklist. (This is the same function from Chapter 3's challenge problem — connect your answer there to the full picture.)
    Domain: all reals. Intercepts: \((0,0)\) and \((2,0)\) (double roots, so the curve touches but doesn't cross at each). From Ch. 3: critical points at \(x=0,1,2\), classified as local min, local max, local min respectively. \(f''=12x^2-24x+8\): solving \(f''=0\) gives \(x=1\pm1/\sqrt3\), both genuine inflection points flanking the local max at \(x=1\). End behavior: even degree, positive leading coefficient — both ends go up. The full curve is a "W" shape sitting on the x-axis at its two roots.
