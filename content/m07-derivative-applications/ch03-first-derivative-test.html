Chapter 3 · concept:first-derivative-test

The First Derivative Test

Chapter 2 built the shortlist of candidate x-values. This chapter finally answers the question those candidates raise: at each one, is it a peak, a valley, or neither?

Why this matters

Module 1 (Ch. 12) taught increasing/decreasing intervals by eye, off a graph. Now there's a computable version of exactly the same idea: f'>0 means f is increasing there, f'<0 means decreasing — no picture required. This chapter's whole contribution is using Chapter 2's critical points as the boundary markers between those intervals, turning "increasing/decreasing" into a mechanical procedure that classifies every critical point in one pass.

Theorem — The First Derivative Test
\[ \text{at a critical point } c: \begin{cases} f' \text{ goes } {+}\to{-} & \Rightarrow \text{local max} \\ f' \text{ goes } {-}\to{+} & \Rightarrow \text{local min} \\ \text{no sign change} & \Rightarrow \text{neither} \end{cases} \]
(7.3.1)

"Goes + to −" means: pick a test point just to the left of \(c\) and one just to the right, and check the sign of \(f'\) at each. If \(f\) is increasing right up to \(c\) and decreasing right after, \(c\) has to be a peak — a local max. Same logic, mirrored, for a local min.

Worked example — classifying x=0 and x=2 for f(x)=x³−3x²

Chapter 2 found this function's critical points at x=0 and x=2 (from f'(x)=3x²−6x=3x(x−2)). Classify each.

Step 1 — build a sign chart with test points in each interval

Intervals: \((-\infty,0)\), \((0,2)\), \((2,\infty)\). Test points: \(x=-1\), \(x=1\), \(x=3\).

Step 2 — evaluate the sign of f′ at each test point

\(f'(-1)=3(-1)(-3)=9>0\). \(f'(1)=3(1)(-1)=-3<0\). \(f'(3)=3(3)(1)=9>0\).

Step 3 — apply (7.3.1) at each critical point

At \(x=0\): sign goes \(+\to-\) → local max. At \(x=2\): sign goes \(-\to+\) → local min.

Result

\(f(0)=0\): local max at \((0,0)\). \(f(2)=8-12=-4\): local min at \((2,-4)\).

Common mistake

Checking the sign of f' at the critical point itself instead of on either side of it. At a critical point where f'(c)=0, that's exactly what you already knew — zero tells you nothing about which direction the sign is changing. The test lives entirely in the two neighboring intervals, not at the point. Always pick a test value strictly between critical points, never one of the critical points themselves.

Practice

  1. Classify the critical point of f(x) = x² − 4x + 3.
    \(f'=2x-4\): critical at \(x=2\); \(-\) before, \(+\) after → local min
  2. Classify the critical point of f(x) = -x² + 6x.
    \(f'=-2x+6\): critical at \(x=3\); \(+\) before, \(-\) after → local max
  3. Classify both critical points of f(x) = x³ − 3x.
    \(f'=3x^2-3=3(x-1)(x+1)\), critical at \(x=\pm1\). Sign: \(+\) on \((-\infty,-1)\), \(-\) on \((-1,1)\), \(+\) on \((1,\infty)\). \(x=-1\): local max. \(x=1\): local min.
  4. Classify the critical point of f(x) = x³.
    \(f'=3x^2 \geq 0\) everywhere — no sign change at \(x=0\) → neither a max nor a min
  5. Classify all critical points of f(x) = x⁴ − 2x².
    \(f'=4x^3-4x=4x(x-1)(x+1)\), critical at \(x=0,\pm1\). Sign: \(-\) on \((-\infty,-1)\), \(+\) on \((-1,0)\), \(-\) on \((0,1)\), \(+\) on \((1,\infty)\). \(x=-1\): local min. \(x=0\): local max. \(x=1\): local min.
  6. Classify the critical point of f(x) = (x-1)².
    \(f'=2(x-1)\): critical at \(x=1\); \(-\) before, \(+\) after → local min
  7. bonus — Classify both critical points of f(x) = x³ − 6x² + 9x.
    \(f'=3x^2-12x+9=3(x-1)(x-3)\), critical at \(x=1,3\). Sign: \(+\), then \(-\) on \((1,3)\), then \(+\). \(x=1\): local max. \(x=3\): local min.
  8. bonus — Suppose f' is negative on (−∞,2), positive on (2,5), and negative on (5,∞). Classify x=2 and x=5 without knowing f's formula.
    \(x=2\): \(-\to+\) → local min. \(x=5\): \(+\to-\) → local max. (The theorem only needs the sign chart, not the formula.)
  9. bonus — Classify the critical point of f(x) = x^{1/3} at x=0 using the sign of f′.
    \(f'(x)=1/(3x^{2/3})\) is positive on both sides of \(0\) (the denominator is a square, always positive for \(x\neq0\)) — no sign change → neither a max nor a min, just a vertical-tangent point on an otherwise increasing curve.
  10. bonus — For the constant function f(x) = 5, what does the First Derivative Test say about its (infinitely many) critical points?
    \(f'(x)=0\) everywhere, so technically every point is critical — but the sign never changes anywhere (it's always exactly \(0\), never \(+\) or \(-\)), so none of them are local extrema. The function is just flat.
  11. challenge — Fully classify all three critical points of f(x) = x⁴ − 4x³ + 4x² = x²(x-2)².
    \(f'=4x^3-12x^2+8x=4x(x-1)(x-2)\), critical at \(x=0,1,2\). Sign: \(-\) on \((-\infty,0)\), \(+\) on \((0,1)\), \(-\) on \((1,2)\), \(+\) on \((2,\infty)\). \(x=0\): local min. \(x=1\): local max. \(x=2\): local min.
