Chapter 11 · concept:newtons-method

Newton's Method

Chapter 9 used the tangent line to estimate a curve's value near a point. This chapter uses the same tangent line for a different job entirely: walking toward a root of an equation that can't be solved by any algebra trick at all.

Why this matters

The quadratic formula solves ax²+bx+c=0 exactly. Most equations have no such formula — cos(x)=x, for instance, can't be solved algebraically at all. Newton's method sidesteps that entirely: start with a guess, follow the tangent line down to where it crosses the x-axis, treat that as a better guess, and repeat. Each step reuses exactly one piece of Module 6's toolkit (the derivative) and Chapter 9's idea (the tangent line as a local stand-in), and it converges shockingly fast.

Method — Newton's iteration formula
\[ x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)} \]
(7.11.1)

Starting from a guess \(x_0\), this formula produces \(x_1\), then \(x_2\), and so on — each one the x-intercept of the tangent line to \(f\) at the previous guess (Chapter 9's linear approximation, solved for where \(L(x)=0\) instead of estimated at a nearby point). Repeated enough times, \(x_n\) converges toward an actual root of \(f\).

Worked example — approximating √2

Approximate √2 by finding a root of f(x) = x² − 2, starting from x₀ = 1.5.

Step 1 — set up f and f′

\(f(x)=x^2-2\), \(f'(x)=2x\). A root of \(f\) is exactly \(\sqrt2\), since \(f(x)=0 \iff x^2=2\).

Step 2 — first iteration

\(f(1.5)=2.25-2=0.25\), \(f'(1.5)=3\). \(x_1 = 1.5 - \dfrac{0.25}{3} = 1.5-0.0833 = 1.4167\)

Step 3 — second iteration

\(f(1.4167)=1.4167^2-2\approx0.00695\), \(f'(1.4167)\approx2.8333\). \(x_2 = 1.4167 - \dfrac{0.00695}{2.8333} \approx 1.41422\)

Result

The true value \(\sqrt2\approx1.41421\) — already correct to four decimal places after just two iterations. This rapid closing-in is typical of Newton's method: the number of correct digits roughly doubles with each step.

Common mistake

Picking a starting guess far from the actual root, or at a point where f′(x)=0 — the formula (7.11.1) divides by \(f'(x_n)\), so a zero derivative breaks the method instantly (no next iterate is defined). For f(x)=x²-2, starting at x₀=0 gives \(f'(0)=0\) — the method can't even take its first step. A poor starting guess (far from the root, or near a flat spot in the curve) can also converge slowly, jump to a different root than intended, or fail to converge at all — always sanity-check that the starting guess is reasonably close to the root you're actually after.

Try it yourself

Do one iteration of Newton's method for f(x) = x² − 2, starting at x₀ = 2.

Practice

  1. One iteration for f(x) = x² − 5, x₀ = 2. Find x₁.
    \(f(2)=-1\), \(f'(2)=4\). \(x_1=2-(-1)/4=2.25\)
  2. One iteration for f(x) = x² − 10, x₀ = 3. Find x₁.
    \(f(3)=-1\), \(f'(3)=6\). \(x_1=3-(-1)/6\approx3.1667\)
  3. One iteration for f(x) = x³ − 2 (finding ∛2), x₀ = 1.5. Find x₁.
    \(f(1.5)=1.375\), \(f'(1.5)=6.75\). \(x_1=1.5-1.375/6.75\approx1.2963\)
  4. What happens if you start Newton's method for f(x) = x² − 4 at x₀ = 0?
    \(f'(0)=0\) — division by zero, the method breaks immediately with no next iterate defined (see the Common Mistake box).
  5. One iteration for f(x) = x² − 2x − 3, x₀ = 4. Find x₁.
    \(f(4)=5\), \(f'(4)=6\). \(x_1=4-5/6\approx3.1667\) (heading toward the actual root at \(x=3\))
  6. One iteration for f(x) = \cos x - x, x₀ = 0.5 (radians). Find x₁.
    \(f(0.5)=\cos(0.5)-0.5\approx0.3776\), \(f'(x)=-\sin x - 1\), \(f'(0.5)\approx-1.4794\). \(x_1\approx0.5-0.3776/(-1.4794)\approx0.7552\)
  7. bonus — Do two iterations for f(x) = x² − 3 (approximating √3), x₀ = 2.
    \(x_1=2-1/4=1.75\). \(x_2=1.75-0.0625/3.5\approx1.7321\) — matches \(\sqrt3\approx1.7321\)
  8. bonus — One iteration for f(x) = x² − 2, x₀ = -1.5. What root does this converge toward?
    \(f(-1.5)=0.25\), \(f'(-1.5)=-3\). \(x_1=-1.5-0.25/(-3)\approx-1.4167\) — converging toward \(-\sqrt2\) instead, since the starting guess was negative
  9. bonus — Explain geometrically what x_{n+1} represents, relative to x_n.
    \(x_{n+1}\) is the x-intercept of the tangent line to \(f\) at \(x_n\) — each step follows that tangent line down (or up) to where it crosses the x-axis, and uses that crossing point as the next guess.
  10. bonus — One iteration for f(x) = x³ − x − 2, x₀ = 1.5. Find x₁.
    \(f(1.5)=-0.125\), \(f'(1.5)=5.75\). \(x_1=1.5-(-0.125)/5.75\approx1.5217\)
  11. challenge — Use two iterations of Newton's method to approximate the root of f(x) = x³ − x − 1, starting from x₀ = 1.
    \(f(1)=-1\), \(f'(1)=2\). \(x_1=1-(-1)/2=1.5\). \(f(1.5)=0.875\), \(f'(1.5)=5.75\). \(x_2=1.5-0.875/5.75\approx1.3478\) — closing in on the true root, \(\approx1.3247\).
