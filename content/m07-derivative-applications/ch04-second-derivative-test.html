Concavity & the Second Derivative Test

Chapter 3 classified critical points by checking f′'s sign on either side — a two-sided investigation. This chapter gets the same answer from a single number, most of the time, by differentiating one more time.

Why this matters

f' describes whether the graph is going up or down. f'' — the derivative of the derivative — describes whether that rate of change is itself increasing or decreasing, which is exactly what "curving up like a bowl" versus "curving down like a dome" means. It's a second layer of the same idea Module 6 already handed you: just differentiate again.

Definition — Concavity and inflection points
\[ f \text{ is concave up on } I \iff f''(x)>0 \text{ on } I; \qquad f \text{ is concave down on } I \iff f''(x)<0 \text{ on } I \]
(7.4.1)

Concave up looks like a bowl (∪-shaped locally); concave down looks like a dome (∩-shaped locally). An inflection point is a point where concavity actually switches — from up to down or down to up — which typically happens where \(f''(x)=0\) or is undefined, and the sign genuinely changes there (just like a critical point needed an actual sign change in \(f'\) to be an extremum).

Theorem — The Second Derivative Test
\[ \text{if } f'(c)=0: \begin{cases} f''(c)>0 & \Rightarrow \text{local min} \\ f''(c)<0 & \Rightarrow \text{local max} \\ f''(c)=0 & \Rightarrow \text{inconclusive — fall back to (7.3.1)} \end{cases} \]
(7.4.2)

Faster than Chapter 3's test when it applies — one number, no sign chart — but it only applies at a critical point where \(f'(c)=0\) (not the "undefined derivative" kind from Chapter 2), and it says nothing at all when \(f''(c)=0\). That case isn't a special exception to memorize; it's a genuine dead end that needs the First Derivative Test instead.

Worked example — reclassifying f(x)=x³−3x², and its inflection point

Confirm Chapter 3's classification of f(x) = x³ − 3x²'s critical points using the Second Derivative Test, then find its inflection point.

Step 1 — find f′ and f″

\(f'(x)=3x^2-6x\), \(f''(x)=6x-6\).

Step 2 — evaluate f″ at each critical point (x=0, x=2, from Ch. 2)

\(f''(0)=-6<0 \Rightarrow\) local max at \(x=0\). \(f''(2)=6>0 \Rightarrow\) local min at \(x=2\). Both match Chapter 3's sign-chart answer, exactly as they must.

Step 3 — find the inflection point by solving f″(x)=0

\(6x-6=0 \Rightarrow x=1\).

Step 4 — confirm the sign of f″ actually changes there

\(f''<0\) for \(x<1\) (concave down), \(f''>0\) for \(x>1\) (concave up) — a genuine switch.

Result

Inflection point at \(x=1\), \(f(1)=1-3=-2\): the point \((1,-2)\) — exactly where the curve stops caving downward and starts curving upward, right between the local max and the local min.

Common mistake

Applying the Second Derivative Test when f''(c)=0 and forcing out a conclusion anyway. Take f(x)=x⁴ at x=0: \(f'(x)=4x^3\), so \(f'(0)=0\) — a critical point. \(f''(x)=12x^2\), so \(f''(0)=0\) too — genuinely inconclusive by (7.4.2), not "probably neither" or "probably a saddle." Falling back to the First Derivative Test settles it: \(f'\) is negative for \(x<0\) and positive for \(x>0\), a clean \(-\to+\) sign change, so \(x=0\) actually is a local min — despite \(f''(0)=0\) giving no direct signal either way. When (7.4.2) hands back "inconclusive," that's the whole answer for that test; it isn't a hint toward any particular classification.

Practice

  1. Find f″ for f(x) = x² − 6x and state its concavity everywhere.
    \(f''=2\), concave up everywhere
  2. Find f″ for f(x) = -2x² + 3x and state its concavity everywhere.
    \(f''=-4\), concave down everywhere
  3. Find the inflection point of f(x) = x³ − 6x² + 9x.
    \(f''=6x-12=0 \Rightarrow x=2\); \(f(2)=8-24+18=2\) → \((2,2)\)
  4. Classify the critical points of f(x) = x³ − 3x using the Second Derivative Test.
    \(f'=3x^2-3=0 \Rightarrow x=\pm1\). \(f''=6x\). \(f''(1)=6>0\) → local min. \(f''(-1)=-6<0\) → local max.
  5. Find the inflection point(s) of f(x) = x⁴ − 8x².
    \(f''=12x^2-16=0 \Rightarrow x^2=4/3 \Rightarrow x=\pm2/\sqrt3\) (both are genuine sign changes, by symmetry)
  6. Classify the critical point of f(x) = x² + 4x + 1 using the Second Derivative Test.
    \(f'=2x+4=0 \Rightarrow x=-2\); \(f''=2>0\) → local min
  7. bonus — For f(x) = x⁴, show the Second Derivative Test is inconclusive at x=0, then classify it using the First Derivative Test instead.
    \(f''(0)=0\) → inconclusive. \(f'(x)=4x^3\) is \(-\) for \(x<0\), \(+\) for \(x>0\) → local min (see the Common Mistake box).
  8. bonus — Is x=0 an inflection point of f(x) = x³? Justify using f″.
    Yes: \(f''=6x\), negative for \(x<0\), positive for \(x>0\) — a genuine sign change at \((0,0)\). (Also a critical point with no extremum there, from Ch. 3 — both facts hold at once.)
  9. bonus — Does f(x) = x² − 4 have any inflection points? Explain.
    No — \(f''=2>0\) for every \(x\), so it's concave up everywhere and concavity never switches.
  10. bonus — Given f''(x) = (x-1)(x-3) directly, find all inflection points and describe the concavity on each interval.
    Zeros at \(x=1,3\). Sign: \(+\) on \((-\infty,1)\) (concave up), \(-\) on \((1,3)\) (concave down), \(+\) on \((3,\infty)\) (concave up). Both \(x=1\) and \(x=3\) are inflection points.
  11. challenge — For f(x) = x⁴ − 4x³ + 6, find all critical points (classifying each — noting where the Second Derivative Test is inconclusive) and all inflection points.
    \(f'=4x^3-12x^2=4x^2(x-3)\), critical at \(x=0,3\). \(f''=12x^2-24x=12x(x-2)\). \(f''(0)=0\) → inconclusive; First Derivative Test: \(f'(x)=4x^2(x-3)\) is negative on both sides of \(0\) (the \(x^2\) never flips sign) → neither max nor min at \(x=0\). \(f''(3)=36>0\) → local min at \(x=3\). Inflection points: \(f''=0\) at \(x=0,2\); sign of \(f''=12x(x-2)\) is \(+\) before \(0\), \(-\) between \(0\) and \(2\), \(+\) after \(2\) — both are genuine switches, so \(x=0\) and \(x=2\) are both inflection points (notice \(x=0\) is simultaneously a non-extreme critical point and an inflection point).
