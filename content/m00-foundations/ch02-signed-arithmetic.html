Chapter 2 · concept:signed-arithmetic

Signed Numbers & Distribution

Yesterday you fixed one sign slip. This chapter makes every sign rule automatic, so it stops being a source of errors anywhere downstream.

Why this matters

Every algebra mistake that isn't conceptual is almost always a sign error. Derivatives, factoring, solving equations — all of it sits on top of "can I reliably combine positive and negative numbers without thinking hard." We make it automatic now, with a physical picture (the number line) backing up the abstract rule.

Adding and subtracting

Definition — Subtracting a negative
\[ a - (-b) = a + b \]
(0.2.1)

Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its positive. On a number line: subtraction normally moves you left; subtracting a negative flips that, so you move right.

−4 − (−2) = −4 + 2 = −2

Multiplying and dividing

Rule — Sign of a product or quotient

Same signs → positive. Different signs → negative. This holds for both multiplication and division:

\[ (+)(+) = +, \quad (-)(-) = +, \quad (+)(-) = -, \quad (-)(+) = - \]
(0.2.2)

Shortcut for longer products: count the negative signs. An even number of negatives → positive result. An odd number → negative result.

Worked example

Compute: −7 − (−3)

Step 1 — rewrite subtracting a negative as adding

\( -7 - (-3) = -7 + 3 \)

Step 2 — combine

Start at \(-7\), move 3 right: \(-7+3=-4\)

Worked example

Compute: (−4)(−2) − 6

Step 1 — multiply first (order of operations)

Two negatives multiplied → positive: \( (-4)(-2) = 8 \)

Step 2 — subtract

\( 8 - 6 = 2 \)

Try it yourself

Compute: (−6)(−2) + (−5)

Common mistake

Mixing up "two negatives make a positive" for multiplication with addition. −4 + (−2) = −6 (still negative!) — negatives only cancel out to positive under multiplication/division, or when you're subtracting a negative. Don't apply the multiplication rule to addition.

Practice

  1. Compute: −5 + (−3)
    \(-8\)
  2. Compute: 8 − (−6)
    \(8+6=14\)
  3. Compute: (−5)(3)
    \(-15\)
  4. Compute: (−12) ÷ (−4)
    \(3\)
  5. Compute: −3 − 5 + (−2)(4)
    \(-2\times4=-8\), then \(-3-5-8=-16\)
  6. Compute: (−1)(−1)(−1)(−2)
    Four negatives (even count) → positive: \(2\)
  7. bonus — Compute: −9 − (−9)
    \(0\)
  8. bonus — Compute: (−6)(2) − (−3)(5)
    \(-12-(-15) = -12+15=3\)
  9. bonus — Compute: 15 ÷ (−3) + (−2)(−2)
    \(-5+4=-1\)
  10. bonus — Simplify: −2(x − 3) − (x − 5)
    \(-2x+6-x+5 = -3x+11\)
  11. challenge — Without a calculator, determine the sign only (not the value) of (−3)⁷, and explain your reasoning in one sentence.
    Negative — 7 factors of \(-3\) multiplied together is an odd count of negatives, so the result is negative.
