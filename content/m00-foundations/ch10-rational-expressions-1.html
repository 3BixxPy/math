Rational Expressions I — Simplifying & Domain

All three factoring chapters were building toward this: a fraction of polynomials only simplifies by canceling factors, and every value that would divide by zero has to be tracked and excluded — a skill that comes back constantly once you reach functions.

Why this matters

"Domain" — which inputs are actually allowed — becomes a central idea starting next module (Functions & Graphs). Rational expressions are the cleanest place to build the habit: division by zero is undefined, full stop, so any value making a denominator zero must be excluded, even if it later cancels out algebraically.

Definition — Rational expression
\[ \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}, \quad Q(x) \neq 0 \]
(0.10.1)

A ratio of two polynomials. Just like a plain fraction, the bottom can never be zero.

Rule — Simplifying: cancel factors, never terms

You may only cancel something that is a factor of the entire numerator and the entire denominator — meaning it's multiplied by everything else, not added to it. That's exactly why Chapters 7–9 came first: you need the expression factored before you can safely cancel anything.

Worked example

Simplify (x² − 1)/(x² + 3x + 2), stating all domain restrictions.

Step 1 — factor top and bottom

Top: \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\). Bottom: \(x^2+3x+2=(x+1)(x+2)\).

Step 2 — note the restrictions from the original denominator, before canceling

\((x+1)(x+2)=0\) when \(x=-1\) or \(x=-2\). Both are excluded, permanently — this doesn't change no matter what happens next.

Step 3 — cancel the common factor

\(\dfrac{(x-1)(x+1)}{(x+1)(x+2)} = \dfrac{x-1}{x+2}\)

Result
\[ \frac{x^2-1}{x^2+3x+2} = \frac{x-1}{x+2} \quad (x \neq -1,\, x\neq-2) \]
(0.10.2)

Notice \(x=-1\) doesn't even look excluded in the simplified form anymore — but it still is, because the restriction comes from the original expression, not the simplified one.

Common mistake

Canceling pieces of a sum instead of factors of a product. (x + 3)/(x + 5) does not simplify by "canceling the x's" — the \(x\) is not a factor of the whole numerator or the whole denominator (it's only part of a sum), so nothing can be canceled here at all. Only cancel after full factoring, and only whole factors.

Practice

  1. State the domain restriction: 1/(x − 7)
    \(x\neq7\)
  2. State the domain restrictions: (x+2)/(x² − 9)
    \(x\neq3,\, x\neq-3\)
  3. Simplify, with restriction: (x² − 4)/(x² + 4x + 4)
    \(\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{(x+2)^2}=\dfrac{x-2}{x+2}\), \(x\neq-2\)
  4. Simplify, with restrictions: (x² + x − 6)/(x² − 9)
    \(\dfrac{(x+3)(x-2)}{(x-3)(x+3)}=\dfrac{x-2}{x-3}\), \(x\neq-3,3\)
  5. Simplify, with restriction: (x² − 25)/(x + 5)
    \(x-5\), \(x\neq-5\)
  6. Simplify, with restrictions: (2x² − 8)/(x² − 4)
    \(\dfrac{2(x-2)(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+2)}=2\), \(x\neq2,-2\) — note it simplifies to a plain constant, but the restrictions still apply!
  7. bonus — Simplify, with restrictions: (x² − 6x + 9)/(x² − 9)
    \(\dfrac{(x-3)^2}{(x-3)(x+3)}=\dfrac{x-3}{x+3}\), \(x\neq3,-3\)
  8. bonus — Simplify, with restrictions: (x² + 2x − 15)/(x² − 25)
    \(\dfrac{(x+5)(x-3)}{(x-5)(x+5)}=\dfrac{x-3}{x-5}\), \(x\neq-5,5\)
  9. bonus — Explain why (x+4)/(x+9) cannot be simplified any further.
    Neither numerator nor denominator factors further, and they share no common factor — there's nothing to cancel.
  10. bonus — Simplify, with restriction: (3x² − 3)/(x − 1)
    \(\dfrac{3(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1}=3(x+1)=3x+3\), \(x\neq1\)
  11. challenge — Simplify (x³ − x)/(x² − 1) fully, and explain why a restriction is still needed even though the simplified answer is just x, with no fraction in sight.
    Top: \(x(x-1)(x+1)\). Bottom: \((x-1)(x+1)\). Cancels to \(x\), with \(x\neq1,-1\). The restriction survives because it describes the original expression's domain — the simplified form is only equal to the original everywhere except those excluded points.
