Factoring II — Trinomials (x² + bx + c)

On the diagnostic, you solved x² − 5x + 6 = 0 with the quadratic formula — correctly, but you had to look the formula up. This chapter gives you a faster route for quadratics like this one: spot the factors directly.

Why this matters

Not every quadratic needs the full formula. Many of the ones you'll meet — especially ones built for teaching — factor cleanly, and factoring is faster once you can do it fluently. It also builds the same "what are the roots" intuition you'll need for calculus later, just via a different route.

Rule — Factoring \(x^2+bx+c\)
\[ (x+p)(x+q) = x^2 + (p+q)x + pq \]
(0.8.1)

Reading this backward: to factor \(x^2+bx+c\), find two numbers \(p\) and \(q\) that multiply to \(c\) and add to \(b\). Then the factors are \((x+p)(x+q)\).

Worked example — the exact diagnostic quadratic

Factor: x² − 5x + 6

Step 1 — list factor pairs of c = 6

\(1\times6\), \(2\times3\), \((-1)\times(-6)\), \((-2)\times(-3)\)

Step 2 — find the pair that also sums to b = −5

\(-2 + (-3) = -5\). ✓ Both conditions match with \(p=-2, q=-3\).

Step 3 — write the factors

\((x-2)(x-3)\)

Step 4 — check by expanding back (always do this)

\((x-2)(x-3) = x^2-3x-2x+6 = x^2-5x+6\) ✓ matches the original.

Result — matches your diagnostic answer

Setting each factor to zero: \(x-2=0 \Rightarrow x=2\), \(x-3=0 \Rightarrow x=3\). Same roots \(x=2,3\) you got with the quadratic formula — factoring is just a shortcut to the same place, when it's available.

Worked example — negative constant term

Factor: x² + 2x − 15

Step 1 — factor pairs of c = −15 (one positive, one negative, since c is negative)

Need product \(-15\), sum \(2\): try \(5\) and \(-3\): \(5\times(-3)=-15\) ✓, \(5+(-3)=2\) ✓.

Step 2 — write the factors

\((x+5)(x-3)\)

Common mistake

Getting the multiplication condition right but the sum wrong (or vice versa) — both must hold simultaneously. Also: when \(c\) is negative, \(p\) and \(q\) must have opposite signs (a negative times a positive is negative); when \(c\) is positive, they must have the same sign as each other, matching the sign of \(b\). Always verify with the expand-back check — it takes ten seconds and catches every mistake.

Practice

  1. Factor: x² + 7x + 12
    \((x+3)(x+4)\)
  2. Factor: x² − 3x − 10
    \((x-5)(x+2)\)
  3. Factor: x² − 8x + 16
    \((x-4)^2\) — a perfect square trinomial, same pattern as Chapter 6
  4. Factor: x² + x − 6
    \((x+3)(x-2)\)
  5. Factor: x² − 9x + 20
    \((x-4)(x-5)\)
  6. Factor: x² + 4x − 21
    \((x+7)(x-3)\)
  7. bonus — Factor: x² − 2x − 24
    \((x-6)(x+4)\)
  8. bonus — Factor: x² + 10x + 25
    \((x+5)^2\)
  9. bonus — Factor: x² − x − 42
    \((x-7)(x+6)\)
  10. bonus — Factor: x² + 6x − 16
    \((x+8)(x-2)\)
