Systems of Linear Equations (Two Variables)

Two unknowns, two equations, one shared solution. This is a direct preview of Module 10 (Linear Algebra), where the exact same problem gets solved again with matrices — same idea, more variables.

Why this matters

A "system" just means: find the \(x\) and \(y\) that satisfy both equations at once. Two methods handle every 2-variable linear system; later, when there are 10 unknowns instead of 2, the elimination idea generalizes directly into Gaussian elimination on matrices — you're not learning a disposable trick here, you're learning the seed of a whole module.

Definition — Linear system in two variables
\[ \begin{cases} ax+by = e \\ cx+dy = f \end{cases} \]
(0.14.1)

The solution is the pair \((x,y)\) that makes both equations true simultaneously — geometrically, the point where the two lines cross.

Method 1 — Elimination

Method — Elimination

Add or subtract the two equations (after optionally scaling one or both) so that one variable cancels out entirely, leaving a single equation in one variable.

Worked example

Solve: x + y = 5 and x − y = 1

Step 1 — add the two equations

\((x+y) + (x-y) = 5+1 \Rightarrow 2x = 6\) — the \(y\)'s cancel because one is \(+y\) and the other is \(-y\).

Step 2 — solve for x

\(x=3\)

Step 3 — substitute back to find y

\(3+y=5 \Rightarrow y=2\)

Result
\[ x=3,\ y=2 \]
(0.14.2)

Always check: \(3+2=5\) ✓, \(3-2=1\) ✓.

Method 2 — Substitution

Method — Substitution

Solve one equation for one variable, then substitute that expression into the other equation, collapsing it to one variable.

Worked example

Solve: y = x + 2 and 3x + y = 14

Step 1 — substitute y = x+2 into the second equation

\(3x + (x+2) = 14\)

Step 2 — solve for x

\(4x+2=14 \Rightarrow 4x=12 \Rightarrow x=3\)

Step 3 — back-substitute to find y

\(y = 3+2 = 5\)

Common mistake

When subtracting one full equation from another, the minus sign must distribute across every term on the right-hand side too — not just the first one. E.g. eliminating \(x\) from 2x+3y=12 and 2x−y=4 by subtracting: \((2x+3y)-(2x-y) = 3y-(-y) = 3y+y=4y\), not \(3y-y\). Forgetting to distribute the subtraction sign to the second term is the single most common elimination error.

Practice

  1. Solve by elimination: x + y = 9, x − y = 3
    \(x=6, y=3\)
  2. Solve by substitution: y = 2x − 1, x + y = 11
    \(x=4, y=7\)
  3. Solve by elimination: 3x + y = 10, x + y = 4
    Subtract: \(2x=6 \Rightarrow x=3\), \(y=1\)
  4. Solve by substitution: x = y + 3, 2x + y = 12
    \(y=2, x=5\)
  5. Solve by elimination: 2x + 3y = 13, 2x − y = 1
    Subtract: \(4y=12 \Rightarrow y=3\), \(x=2\)
  6. Solve (pick your method): 4x − y = 7, x + y = 8
    Add: \(5x=15 \Rightarrow x=3\), \(y=5\)
  7. bonus — Solve by elimination: 5x + 2y = 16, 3x − 2y = 0
    Add: \(8x=16 \Rightarrow x=2\), \(y=3\)
  8. bonus — Solve by substitution: y = 3x + 1, 4x − y = −5
    \(x=-4, y=-11\)
  9. bonus — Solve by elimination (scale one equation first): 2x + y = 11, x + 3y = 18
    Scale first by 3: \(6x+3y=33\); subtract second: \(5x=15 \Rightarrow x=3\), \(y=5\)
  10. bonus — Solve by elimination: 3x + 4y = 25, 3x − 2y = 1
    Subtract: \(6y=24 \Rightarrow y=4\), \(x=3\)
  11. challenge — Solve by elimination, scaling both equations: 2x + 5y = 13, 3x − 2y = 10
    Scale first ×2: \(4x+10y=26\); scale second ×5: \(15x-10y=50\); add: \(19x=76 \Rightarrow x=4\); back-substitute: \(y=1\)
