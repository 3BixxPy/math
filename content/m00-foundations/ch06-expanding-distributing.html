Day 6 · concept:foil-expanding-binomials

Expanding & Distributing (FOIL and Beyond)

Factoring — next three chapters — is just this chapter run backward. To reverse a pattern, you first need to be fast at running it forward.

Why this matters

On the diagnostic, (x²−9)/(x−3) was attempted by splitting terms individually instead of recognizing x²−9 as a product of two binomials in disguise. That recognition skill comes from having expanded (x−3)(x+3) enough times that the resulting pattern x²−9 is instantly familiar. This chapter builds that pattern recognition; the next one uses it to factor.

Distributing twice

Rule — Multiplying two binomials (FOIL)
\[ (a+b)(c+d) = ac + ad + bc + bd \]
(0.6.1)

This is just the distributive property applied twice: distribute \(a\) over \((c+d)\), then distribute \(b\) over \((c+d)\). "FOIL" is a memory aid for the four products: First×First, Outer×Outer, Inner×Inner, Last×Last.

Worked example

Expand: (x + 3)(x − 5)

Step 1 — First × First

\(x \cdot x = x^2\)

Step 2 — Outer × Outer

\(x \cdot (-5) = -5x\)

Step 3 — Inner × Inner

\(3 \cdot x = 3x\)

Step 4 — Last × Last

\(3 \cdot (-5) = -15\)

Step 5 — combine like terms

\(x^2 -5x+3x-15 = x^2-2x-15\)

Worked example — the pattern behind the diagnostic

Expand: (x − 3)(x + 3)

Step 1 — FOIL it out

\(x^2 + 3x -3x -9\)

Step 2 — notice the middle terms cancel

\(3x - 3x = 0\), leaving just \(x^2 - 9\).

Result — memorize this shape
\[ (a-b)(a+b) = a^2 - b^2 \]
(0.6.2)

Any "square minus square" — like the \(x^2-9\) from the diagnostic — comes from exactly this product. Next chapter runs this backward: given \(x^2-9\), recognize it instantly as \((x-3)(x+3)\).

Rule — Perfect square trinomials
\[ (a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2 \qquad (a-b)^2 = a^2-2ab+b^2 \]
(0.6.3)

Useful later for completing the square (Chapter 13). Note there's a middle term — it does not disappear here the way it did in the difference-of-squares case.

Common mistake — "the freshman's dream"

\((a+b)^2 \neq a^2+b^2\). This is one of the most common algebra errors there is (it even has a nickname). Check with numbers: \((2+3)^2 = 25\), but \(2^2+3^2 = 13\). Not equal. Always expand the middle term.

Practice

  1. Expand: (x + 2)(x + 7)
    \(x^2+9x+14\)
  2. Expand: (x − 4)(x + 6)
    \(x^2+2x-24\)
  3. Expand: (x + 5)(x − 5)
    \(x^2-25\)
  4. Expand: (x + 4)²
    \(x^2+8x+16\)
  5. Expand: (2x − 3)²
    \(4x^2-12x+9\)
  6. Expand: (3x + 1)(2x − 5)
    \(6x^2-15x+2x-5 = 6x^2-13x-5\)
  7. bonus — Expand: (x − 8)(x + 8)
    \(x^2-64\)
  8. bonus — Expand fully from FOIL: (a − b)²
    \(a^2-2ab+b^2\)
  9. bonus — Expand: (x + 7)(x − 7)
    \(x^2-49\)
  10. bonus — Expand: (5 − x)(5 + x)
    \(25-x^2\)
  11. challenge — Expand (a+b)² from scratch using FOIL (not the memorized formula), then explain in one sentence exactly why "\(a^2+b^2\)" is missing something.
    \((a+b)(a+b) = a^2+ab+ab+b^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2\). Skipping to \(a^2+b^2\) drops the two cross terms \(ab+ab=2ab\) that FOIL's Outer and Inner steps produce.
