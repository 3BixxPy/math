Factoring III — Leading Coefficient & Grouping

Chapter 8 handled x² + bx + c. Real quadratics often have a number in front of the x² too — this chapter extends the same idea to handle that, plus a technique (grouping) that generalizes further than quadratics.

Why this matters

The "find two numbers" trick from Chapter 8 doesn't directly work once there's a leading coefficient, because the simple sum/product relationship breaks. The fix (the "ac method" below) is a small, mechanical extension — same core idea, one extra step. Grouping is the more general skill underneath it, and it'll show up again factoring higher-degree polynomials later.

The "ac method" for ax² + bx + c

Method — Factoring when a ≠ 1
  1. Compute \(a \times c\).
  2. Find two numbers that multiply to \(ac\) and add to \(b\) (same search as Chapter 8, just using \(ac\) instead of \(c\)).
  3. Split the middle term \(bx\) into two terms using those numbers.
  4. Factor the resulting four-term expression by grouping (below).
Rule — Factoring by grouping
\[ ax+ay+bx+by = a(x+y)+b(x+y) = (x+y)(a+b) \]
(0.9.1)

Group the four terms in pairs, pull the GCF out of each pair separately, and if you did the pairing right, the same binomial appears in both — factor that binomial out.

Worked example — ac method

Factor: 2x² + 7x + 3

Step 1 — compute ac

\(a=2, c=3\), so \(ac=6\).

Step 2 — find two numbers multiplying to 6, summing to 7

\(6\) and \(1\): \(6\times1=6\) ✓, \(6+1=7\) ✓

Step 3 — split the middle term

\(2x^2 + 6x + x + 3\)

Step 4 — factor by grouping

\((2x^2+6x) + (x+3) = 2x(x+3) + 1(x+3) = (x+3)(2x+1)\)

Check

\((x+3)(2x+1) = 2x^2+x+6x+3 = 2x^2+7x+3\) ✓

Worked example — grouping with a sign trap

Factor: x³ − 3x² − 2x + 6

Step 1 — group in pairs

\((x^3-3x^2) - (2x-6)\) — note the minus sign pulled out front of the second group, which flips the inner sign from \(+6\) to \(-6\) inside the parentheses.

Step 2 — factor GCF from each group

\(x^2(x-3) - 2(x-3)\)

Step 3 — factor the common binomial

\((x-3)(x^2-2)\)

Common mistake

When the second group has a leading minus sign, factoring out a negative GCF flips the sign of every term inside — it's easy to flip only one and not the other. Always re-expand after grouping to check both pairs still match the original expression before continuing.

Practice

  1. Factor: 3x² + 11x + 6
    \((x+3)(3x+2)\)
  2. Factor: 2x² − 5x − 3
    \((x-3)(2x+1)\)
  3. Factor: 5x² + 13x − 6
    \((x+3)(5x-2)\)
  4. Factor by grouping: x³ + 2x² + 3x + 6
    \((x+2)(x^2+3)\)
  5. Factor by grouping: 2x³ − 4x² + 3x − 6
    \((x-2)(2x^2+3)\)
  6. Factor: 4x² + 4x − 3
    \((2x+3)(2x-1)\)
  7. bonus — Factor: 6x² − 7x − 3
    \((2x-3)(3x+1)\)
  8. bonus — Factor by grouping, then keep going: x³ − 5x² − 4x + 20
    \((x-5)(x^2-4) = (x-5)(x-2)(x+2)\) — difference of squares appears after grouping
  9. bonus — Factor: 3x² − 2x − 8
    \((x-2)(3x+4)\)
  10. bonus — Factor by grouping: 6x³ + 9x² + 4x + 6
    \((2x+3)(3x^2+2)\)
