Matrix Addition & Scalar Multiplication

Chapter 2 pointed out that a vector is just a one-row or one-column matrix. Fittingly, the two simplest matrix operations are the exact same two operations Module 9 Chapter 2 taught for vectors — just applied to a bigger grid.

Why this matters

These two operations are deliberately unglamorous: add matching entries, or multiply every entry by the same number. There's no new idea here beyond what vector addition and scalar multiplication already taught — the payoff is that this structural echo keeps repeating throughout linear algebra: matrices generalize vectors, and most "new" matrix operations turn out to be the vector version applied row by row or column by column.

Rule — Matrix addition
\[ (A+B)_{ij} = a_{ij} + b_{ij} \]
(10.3.1)

Add entry-by-entry, in the same position. This is only defined when \(A\) and \(B\) have the exact same dimensions — you cannot add a \(2\times3\) matrix to a \(3\times2\) matrix, or even to a \(2\times2\) matrix, no matter how the numbers might line up. Subtraction works the same way, entry-by-entry, with the same dimension requirement.

Rule — Scalar multiplication
\[ (kA)_{ij} = k \cdot a_{ij} \]
(10.3.2)

Multiply every single entry by the scalar \(k\). Unlike addition, scalar multiplication is always defined — there's no dimension requirement to check, since it only involves one matrix.

Worked example

Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\), \(B = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix}\). Find \(A+B\) and \(-1 \cdot A\).

Step 1 — add matching entries for A+B

\(\begin{bmatrix} 1+5 & 2+6 \\ 3+7 & 4+8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 8 \\ 10 & 12 \end{bmatrix}\)

Step 2 — scalar multiply every entry of A by −1

\(-1\cdot A = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -2 \\ -3 & -4 \end{bmatrix}\) — every sign flips, since every entry got multiplied by \(-1\). This is also how matrix subtraction is defined under the hood: \(A-B = A + (-1)B\).

Common mistake

Trying to add matrices of different dimensions — for example attempting \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}5&6&7\\8&9&10\end{bmatrix}\) (\(2\times2\) plus \(2\times3\)). This is simply undefined, not "a matrix with some entries missing" — there is no matching entry in the first matrix for the third column of the second. Contrast this with scalar multiplication, which never has a dimension requirement at all: \(kA\) is always defined for any matrix \(A\) and any number \(k\). Before adding two matrices, always check dimensions match first — it takes one second and prevents a nonsense answer.

Try it yourself

Compute \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}5&6\\7&8\end{bmatrix}\). What is the entry in row 2, column 2, of the sum?

Practice

  1. Compute \(\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\0&3\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}4&5\\-2&1\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}6&4\\-2&4\end{bmatrix}\)
  2. Compute \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\)
  3. Compute \(3\begin{bmatrix}1&-2\\0&5\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}3&-6\\0&15\end{bmatrix}\)
  4. Compute \(-2\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\-1&3\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}-8&0\\2&-6\end{bmatrix}\)
  5. Is \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2&3\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}4\\5\\6\end{bmatrix}\) defined? Explain.
    No — the first is \(1\times3\) (row vector), the second is \(3\times1\) (column vector); different dimensions, so addition is undefined even though both have three numbers.
  6. Compute \(2\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}3&1\\-1&2\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\-1&2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}5&1\\-1&4\end{bmatrix}\)
  7. bonus — Compute \(\begin{bmatrix}0&1&2\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}5&-1&3\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}5&0&5\end{bmatrix}\)
  8. bonus — Compute \(\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}4&6\\-8&2\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}2&3\\-4&1\end{bmatrix}\)
  9. bonus — Is \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\\5&6\end{bmatrix}\) defined? Explain.
    No — \(2\times2\) vs \(3\times2\); different number of rows.
  10. bonus — Compute \(\begin{bmatrix}2&-3\\1&0\end{bmatrix} - 2\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\)
    \(\begin{bmatrix}2&-3\\1&0\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}2&2\\2&2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-5\\-1&-2\end{bmatrix}\)
  11. challenge — If \(A+B\) is defined and \(A\) is \(3\times5\), what must be true of \(B\)'s dimensions? If additionally \(2A-B\) must also be defined, does that add any further requirement?
    \(B\) must also be \(3\times5\) — addition requires identical dimensions. \(2A\) is automatically \(3\times5\) too (scalar multiplication never changes dimensions), so \(2A-B\) needs no further requirement beyond the one already stated.
